# T-016 · P15 Render

Text for T-016a–e. Published 2026-09-27; this file does not change after publication. Fix lists are written in `tasks/TASKS.md` under the task.

## Preamble

**Goal of P15.** Turn a planned and narrated episode into a video.
- A pure timeline places every part of the episode: the cold open, the title, a slot for each bridge, the shots with their own sound, the closing and the tease. It keeps the narrator off the person's voice, carries word timings for captions and tells the music when to duck.
- The render package draws that timeline in 9:16 and 16:9 with word-level captions.
- The server builds the render input from what is stored and starts the render through a port, with a fixture renderer for tests and Remotion Lambda for real renders.

Starting renders from `EpisodePipeline` waits for P16. The full clips a render needs are uploaded only when P16's "request clips" step asks the phone for them (D38: working copies are deleted after understanding).

**Decided here (DECISIONS D41).**
- **One owner for time.**
  - `episodeTimeline` in `packages/story/src/render/` decides where everything sits and returns a `RenderManifestV2`. The render package only draws it, and the server only gathers its input (ARCHITECTURE §6).
  - The plan's `lowerThirds[].atMs` was computed without bridge slots, so the timeline recomputes it and ignores the stored value.
- **The order.**
  - Cold open, then the title card (`TITLE_CARD_MS`), the scenes in plan order, the closing card (`CLOSING_MS`), then the tease.
  - A scene whose bridge was kept opens with a bridge slot: 400 ms of lead-in, the clip, 600 ms of tail, with the scene's heading on screen.
  - The narrator speaks only inside a bridge slot or the tease slot, so it never talks over the person. The manifest check proves no narrator speech overlaps an answer.
  - A scene without a kept bridge shows its heading as a small label over its first shot for 2,000 ms (or the shot's length if shorter) and adds no time.
  - The closing card, which carries the "AI-narrated" credit (ARCHITECTURE §7), is always there. A missing closing moment removes only its background.
- **Answers with their own sound, lightly cut.**
  - An answer plays with its sound at full volume: a video answer as video, a voice answer over a card with the question it answered.
  - When its words are exact (not approximate), its span is trimmed to 300 ms before the first word and 500 ms after the last, inside the plan's span ("lightly cut for pauses", VISION §5).
  - Library clips keep their natural sound at a lower level. Photos are silent, with Ken Burns.
- **The narrator's share, again.** Trims and dropped shots shorten the person's time after P14 measured the share. So the timeline checks the cap once more on the final speech. While narrator speech × 4 > all speech, it drops narration in T-015's order: the tease first, then bridges from the last scene back, each with its slot.
- **Missing stays missing (rule 10).**
  - The timeline takes only moments that have media.
  - A shot whose moment is missing is dropped, and a scene with no shots left is dropped with its bridge.
  - A missing cold open, cast name, storyline title or question leaves that part out, never replaced. A name, title or question longer than its field allows is left out, never cut.
  - Any shot or cold open shorter than 500 ms, trimmed or not, is dropped.
  - With no shots at all there is nothing to render (`null`).
  - The server rebuilds the brief when it renders, so a moment deleted or made `localOnly` since planning is never rendered (rule 8).
  - A photo original that Chrome cannot draw (not JPEG, PNG or WebP, for example HEIC) is dropped like a missing one. P16's "request clips" asks the phone for a JPEG.
- **Speech drives captions and music.**
  - Every answer and every narration clip becomes a speech unit on the manifest, with its words in episode time (at most 200 words each; any past that go uncaptioned).
  - Captions are drawn from speech units, as word-level pages via `@remotion/captions` (DESIGN §4). The music ducks under every speech unit, not only the narrator.
  - Words marked approximate get pages without the current-word highlight.
- **Captions people can read.**
  - Each page sits on a solid box of the scrim colour at 0.85 opacity, so both word colours pass WCAG AA (4.5:1) over any footage.
  - The spoken word is in full text colour and the rest in the secondary text colour.
- **Mixing.**
  - Answers and narration at volume 1, clips at 0.4.
  - Music at gain 0.6, ducking to 0.15 with 300 ms ramps, a 1 s fade-in and a 1.5 s fade-out.
  - Remotion does not normalise loudness (its mix is a plain sum), so levels are fixed constants, and listening to a real episode is a device-check item.
- **Media tags.**
  - Video stays on `OffthreadVideo`. iPhones record HEVC by default, and `@remotion/media`'s `Video` cannot decode H.265 during rendering (it falls back to `OffthreadVideo` anyway) and needs CORS on every source.
  - Audio uses `Html5Audio`; `Audio` from `remotion` is its deprecated alias from 4.0.358.
- **Two shapes.**
  - 9:16 (1080×1920) is the episode. 16:9 (1920×1080) is an export of an already rendered episode, made on request, never by default.
  - Portrait fills the frame (cover). Landscape shows portrait footage whole on the background colour (pillarbox), without a blurred copy.
  - The cold open is letterboxed: a true 2.39:1 band in 16:9. In 9:16 a 2.39:1 band would cut a talking person to a strip, so portrait uses bars of 10% of the height at top and bottom.
- **Captions are burned in.** The bundled FFmpeg has no subtitle encoders, so no subtitle track is embedded. Burned-in captions meet WCAG's captions requirement. A sidecar file for the in-app player is later work (P16).
- **Rendering on Lambda from a Worker.**
  - Remotion's Lambda client does not run on Cloudflare Workers (its docs say so, and it imports Node modules). So the Worker calls AWS Lambda's `Invoke` API directly, signed with `aws4fetch`.
  - It sends the same `start` and `status` payloads that Remotion's official Python client sends with a plain synchronous invoke (`remotion-lambda` 4.0.529, `remotionclient.py` and `models.py`). It parses the response the same way: concatenated JSON objects, and the last one counts.
  - The payload must match the deployed function's version exactly, so a Node test compares ours with Remotion's own builder from `@remotion/lambda-client` 4.0.529.
- **Polling, not webhooks.** The render step asks for progress (the `status` routine) instead of receiving Remotion's webhook. That means no public endpoint and no signature secret, and Remotion's webhook has no replay protection.
- **Where media comes from and goes.**
  - Lambda reads each file through a presigned R2 GET URL that lives 15 minutes (ARCHITECTURE §7). The pipeline makes one only for keys under the documentary owner's `u/{userId}/{documentaryId}/` prefix.
  - Lambda writes the finished MP4 straight to R2, through Remotion's `outName` with an `s3OutputProvider` (R2's S3 endpoint).
  - Both need an R2 API token scoped to the media bucket. This revisits D34, which avoided R2 S3 credentials for uploads: renders run outside the Worker, so they cannot use the binding.
  - Remotion strips the keys from its stored render metadata. That metadata, which includes the manifest, stays in the owner's Remotion bucket for one day and is then removed by Remotion's folder-expiry lifecycle rules (`deleteAfter: '1-day'`, which work only on a bucket made with folder expiry on). The output uses `privacy: 'no-acl'`.
- **Keys and versions.**
  - The episode's MP4 is `u/{userId}/{documentaryId}/episodes/{episodeId}/v{renderVersion}.mp4`, and the 16:9 export of that version is `…/v{renderVersion}-wide.mp4`. They live under `u/`, so the `tmp/` rule never deletes them and account deletion finds them by prefix. This replaces the stale `users/{uid}/episodes/…` line in ARCHITECTURE.
  - A 9:16 render takes the next render version. A 16:9 export uses the episode's current one.
  - A render already started or finished for the same episode, plan version and format is never started again.
  - A finished 9:16 render moves the episode's `renderVersion` forward and sets `mp4Key` and `durationMs`. The move to `ready` and the push are P16's.
- **Cost.**
  - Each render's cost (µUSD from the progress's accrued cost estimate, rounded up; nothing when it is missing) is stored on its render row.
  - The episode's one ledger row for step `render`, provider `remotionLambda`, unit `render` is the sum over its renders, with units = how many. It is written again after every render, then `costCents`.
  - The Remotion licence is free while the company has at most 3 people (D9). A per-render licence cost is added when the owner says the team has grown.

**Stack (checked 2026-09-27).**
- **`@remotion/captions`** (Remotion 4.0.529; all Remotion packages pinned exactly, D25):
  - `Caption { text, startMs, endMs, timestampMs: number | null, confidence: number | null }`.
  - `createTikTokStyleCaptions({ captions, combineTokensWithinMilliseconds, breakOnSilenceAfterMilliseconds? }) → { pages: { text, startMs, durationMs, tokens: { text, fromMs, toMs }[] }[] }`.
  - A new page starts only at a caption whose `text` begins with a space, and only once the current page spans more than `combineTokensWithinMilliseconds`. So a page runs past that limit by up to one word (ten 300 ms words at 1,200 ms make two pages of five). Every word after the first therefore carries its leading space.
  - A page's `durationMs` runs until the next page starts, so it covers silences, and the caller caps it.
  - The container needs `white-space: pre-wrap`, which keeps the spaces and still wraps.
  - The package is MIT, has no dependencies and runs in Node and in a composition.
- **`OffthreadVideo`:**
  - `muted`; `volume` (a number or `(frame) => number`, with frame 0 when the media starts; `trimBefore` does not shift it); `trimBefore`/`trimAfter` in frames.
  - There is no `loop` (use `<Loop>`).
  - `Html5Audio` has the same volume semantics. A volume of 100 or more throws. The renderer rounds a volume curve to steps of about 0.01.
- **`calculateMetadata`:** `({ props }) → { durationInFrames, width, height, fps }`, run once per render. `renderStill` renders one frame to PNG.
- **Lambda.** `@remotion/lambda` 4.0.529 is for the deploy script only.
  - `getOrCreateBucket({ region, enableFolderExpiry: true })` → `{ bucketName }`.
  - `deploySite({ entryPoint, bucketName, region, siteName })` → `{ serveUrl }`.
  - `deployFunction({ region, timeoutInSeconds 15–900, memorySizeInMb 512–10240, diskSizeInMb (default 2048), createCloudWatchLogGroup, cloudWatchLogRetentionPeriodInDays })` → `{ functionName }`. It runs on arm64.
  - Start and status go through `POST https://lambda.{region}.amazonaws.com/2015-03-31/functions/{name}/invocations` (synchronous). Payloads over about 200 KB would need S3; ours are far smaller and are refused above 200 KB.
  - Progress gives `done`, `fatalErrorEncountered`, `errors`, `overallProgress`, `costs.accruedSoFar` (USD) and `outputSizeInBytes`.
  - An output `key` must match `/^[0-9a-zA-Z-!_.*'()/]+$/`.
  - The account's Lambda concurrency quota can block a first render (`npx remotion lambda quotas`).
- **`aws4fetch` 1.0.20** (MIT, SigV4 in Workers), imported only under `apps/api/src/providers/**`.
  - A presigned GET puts `X-Amz-Expires=900` on the URL, then `sign(url, { aws: { signQuery: true } })`.
  - `AwsClient` retries by default, so the Lambda client is built with `retries: 0`: a start is never sent twice.
- **Known issue.** Open issue remotion#11704 reports audio about 43 ms late in rendered MP4s. It is not fixed in 4.0.529 and is noted for the device check.

## T-016a · The episode timeline · needs T-014a, T-015a

**Goal.** A pure function places every part of an episode on one timeline and returns a checked `RenderManifestV2`.

**Do.**
1. **The contract.** `packages/contracts/src/render/renderManifest.ts` gains `RenderManifestV2`. Keep `KenBurns`; `RenderManifestV1` stays until T-016b.
   - `version: 2`; `format: { width: 1080, height: 1920 } | { width: 1920, height: 1080 }`; `fps: 30`; `durationMs` 1..600000.
   - `segments` 1..40, each `{ fromMs, toMs }` plus one of:
     - `coldOpen { media }`;
     - `title { label 1..40, text 1..60, subtitle? 1..80 }`;
     - `sceneOpen { heading 1..60, background? }`;
     - `shot { media }`;
     - `closing { text 1..80, credit: 'AI-narrated', background? }`;
     - `tease { label 1..40, storyline? 1..60 }`.
   - `media` is one of:
     - `{ type: 'video', src, inMs ≥ 0, sound: 'full' | 'natural' | 'none' }`;
     - `{ type: 'voice', src, inMs ≥ 0, question? 1..200 }`;
     - `{ type: 'photo', src, kenBurns? }`.
   - `background` is `{ type: 'photo', src, kenBurns? } | { type: 'video', src, inMs }` (muted).
   - `narration` 0..6 × `{ src, atMs, durationMs ≥ 200 }`.
   - `speech` 0..40 × `{ kind: 'person' | 'narrator', fromMs, toMs, captions: boolean, approximate: boolean, words 0..200 × { text 1..60, fromMs, toMs } }`.
   - `sceneLabels` 0..5 × `{ atMs, durationMs, text 1..60 }`.
   - `lowerThirds` 0..20 × `{ atMs, durationMs 1000..6000, name 1..40, relation? 1..40 }`.
   - `music?: { src, gain 0..1, duckTo 0..1 }`.
   - Every `toMs` is greater than its `fromMs`.
2. **`shotMs`.** Widen `shotMs` in `packages/story/src/plan/shots.ts` to take `Pick<BriefMoment, 'kind' | 'durationMs'>`. Its results do not change.
3. **The input.** `packages/story/src/render/episodeTimeline.ts` exports `episodeTimeline(input) → RenderManifestV2 | null`, where the input is:
   - `plan` (`EpisodePlanV1`) and `format: 'portrait' | 'landscape'`;
   - `moments`: only the moments that have media, each `{ momentId, kind: 'answer' | 'clip' | 'photo', media: { type: 'video' | 'voice' | 'photo', src, durationMs? }, question?, words?: { words: TimedWord[], approximate } }`;
   - `cast: { id, name, relation? }[]`, `storylines: { id, title }[]`, `episodeNumber`, `weekStart`, `weekEnd`;
   - `narration`: the kept clips, `{ kind: 'bridge' | 'tease', sceneIndex?, src, durationMs, words: TimedWord[] }[]`;
   - `music?: { src }`.
4. **Building the timeline**, in this order:
   - **Cold open.** Take the plan's span, trimmed (below) when the moment is an answer. Media is `sound: 'full'` for a video answer and `'natural'` for a clip; a photo lasts `shotMs`.
   - **Title** (`TITLE_CARD_MS`): `label` = `episodeWords.label(n)` ("Episode 12"), `text` = the plan's title, and `subtitle` = the plan's subtitle or `episodeWords.dates(weekStart, weekEnd)`.
   - **Each scene:**
     - First the bridge slot, if its clip was kept: 400 + clip + 600 ms. Its `background` is the scene's first photo, else its first clip, else none.
     - Then its shots. An answer's span is the shot's span or `[0, durationMs]`, trimmed. A clip's span is `inMs`..`outMs` from the shot, or `shotMs`. A photo lasts `shotMs`, with the shot's `kenBurns` or 1→1.06.
   - **Closing** (`CLOSING_MS`, always): `text` = `episodeWords.closing` and the credit, with the closing moment's photo, clip or video answer as a muted background when it has media.
   - **Tease**, if its clip was kept: 400 + clip + 600 ms, with `label` = `episodeWords.next` and `storyline` = the tease storyline's title.
5. **Placement rules.**
   - **Trim** (answers with exact words only): the span becomes [max(start, first word − 300), min(end, last word + 500)], using the words inside the span.
   - **Drops** follow the Missing line of the preamble.
   - **Speech.**
     - Each answer segment (cold open or shot) gets a `person` unit over the segment. Its words are the ones inside its span, shifted to episode time, clamped to the segment, and kept to the first 200.
     - `captions` is the scene's `captionsFromTranscript`, and true for the cold open.
     - Each narration clip gets a `narrator` unit over the clip, with `captions: true`, and a narration entry at its slot's start + 400.
   - **The share.** While narrator speech × 4 > all speech, drop narration as in the preamble.
   - **Scene labels.** A scene without a kept bridge gets a label at its first shot's start.
   - **Lower thirds.**
     - Each plan lower third starts at its moment's first scene shot + 500 ms and lasts min(3,000, that shot − 500) ms.
     - It is dropped when that is under 1,000 ms, when the cast id is unknown, or when it would overlap an earlier one.
   - **Music.** Gain 0.6 and `duckTo` 0.15, only when `music` is given.
   - **Result.** Parse with the contract; return `null` when no shot is left.
6. **`manifestErrors(m) → string[]`.** It reports:
   - segments that are not contiguous from 0 to `durationMs`, or shorter than 500 ms;
   - anything other than exactly one title and exactly one closing after every shot;
   - a cold open that is not first, or a tease that is not last;
   - narration outside a `sceneOpen` or `tease` segment;
   - overlapping speech units (so no narrator unit overlaps a person unit);
   - narrator speech over a quarter of all speech;
   - a word outside its unit;
   - lower thirds or scene labels that end after `durationMs`, and lower thirds that overlap.

   `episodeTimeline` throws when its own result has errors.
7. **Music and duration.** `musicVolumeAt(ms, m)` and `manifestDurationMs(m)` in `timeline.ts` take `RenderManifestV1 | RenderManifestV2` and branch on `version`. For v2:
   - `musicVolumeAt` gives the gain, ducked to `duckTo` under every speech unit with 300 ms ramps, times a fade-in over the first 1,000 ms and a fade-out over the last 1,500 ms. It is 0 without music.
   - `manifestDurationMs` is `m.durationMs`.
8. **Words.** `packages/story/src/words/episode.ts` defines `episodeWords`, covered by the words test:
   - `label(n)` → "Episode n";
   - `dates(weekStart, weekEnd)` → "12 to 18 October", or "29 September to 5 October" when the months differ;
   - `closing`: "See you next week.";
   - `next`: "Next week".

**Tests.**
- **Order and lengths.** A plan with a cold open, three scenes (bridges kept for scenes 1 and 3), a closing, a tease and two lower thirds gives segments in the right order and lengths, with narration inside the slots, and `manifestErrors` is empty.
- **Trimming.** An answer with exact words is trimmed to 300 ms before and 500 ms after its words; approximate words leave it untrimmed.
- **The narrator's share after trims.** A plan whose narration fits P14's share but whose answers trim short enough to break the cap loses the tease (and then the last bridge) with its slot.
- **Drops.**
  - A shot whose moment is missing is dropped.
  - A scene with no shots left is dropped with its bridge.
  - A 300 ms clip is dropped.
  - A missing cold-open moment gives no cold open.
  - A missing closing moment still ends in a closing segment with the credit and no background.
  - A tease whose storyline is unknown has no storyline line.
  - No media at all gives `null`.
- **Lower thirds.** A lower third lands 500 ms into its moment's first shot after a bridge slot; an unknown cast id is dropped.
- **Speech units.** A unit with 250 words keeps 200.
- **`manifestErrors`** flags hand-made manifests with narration overlapping a person unit, a gap between segments, a missing closing, and a narrator share over a quarter.
- **Music.** `musicVolumeAt` is at `duckTo` in the middle of an answer and of a bridge, at gain between speech, and 0 at 0 ms and at the end. The v1 results are unchanged.
- **Format and words.** The 16:9 format carries the 1920×1080 size. `episodeWords.dates` works within a month and across two.
- **Contract.** It refuses a word that ends before it starts, and 41 segments.
- **`shotMs`.** Its existing tests pass unchanged.

**Acceptance.** Contracts and story typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and the words test pass; `packages/story` still imports only `@life/contracts` and `zod`.

## T-016b · The episode, drawn · needs T-016a

**Goal.** The render package draws a v2 manifest in both shapes with every part's sound where it belongs, and CI proves it on fixtures.

**Do.**
1. **Composition.** In `Root.tsx`, the `Episode` composition takes `RenderManifestV2` (as its schema). Its `calculateMetadata` returns the manifest's width and height, 30 fps and `msToFrames(durationMs)`.
2. **Segments.** `Episode.tsx` draws each segment in a `Sequence`:
   - `ColdOpen`: the media with its sound, and the letterbox (bars of 10% of the height in 9:16, a 2.39:1 band in 16:9).
   - `TitleCard`: the existing title card with `label`, `text` and `subtitle`.
   - `SceneOpen`: the heading as a small tracked label (DESIGN §4) over the background, dimmed, or over the background colour.
   - `Shot`:
     - a video plays with `sound` `full` (volume 1), `natural` (0.4) or `none` (muted), starting at `inMs`;
     - a voice answer plays its audio (`Html5Audio`) under a card showing `question` in the display face;
     - a photo gets Ken Burns.
   - `Closing`: text and credit over the dimmed background.
   - `Tease`: label and storyline.
3. **Overlays and audio.** After the segments come the scene labels, lower thirds and grain, the narration (`Html5Audio` in `Sequence`s) and the music (`Html5Audio`, with volume from `musicVolumeAt`).
   - Media fit comes from `useVideoConfig()`: cover in 9:16, contain on the background colour in 16:9.
   - Reduced motion keeps Ken Burns and the reveals still, as today.
4. **Remove v1.**
   - Remove `RenderManifestV1`, its v1 story helpers (`shotStartsMs`, the v1 `validateTimeline`, the v1 branches of `musicVolumeAt` and `manifestDurationMs`) and their tests. Nothing stored uses v1.
   - `fixtureManifest(format)` now returns `episodeTimeline` over fixture input:
     - a cold open (a video answer);
     - a scene with a kept bridge, a video answer and a photo;
     - a scene without a bridge, with a clip and a voice answer;
     - a lower third, the closing, a kept tease and music.
5. **Fixture media.** `make-fixtures.mts` also renders `answer.mp4` (the fixture clip's picture with `voice.wav`'s sound, 4 s) and writes `answer-voice.wav` (the voice tone, 4 s).
6. **The fixture render.** `render-fixture.mts` renders both formats and checks each:
   - the size;
   - the duration, within ±200 ms of `durationMs`;
   - that an audio stream is present.
   - **Loudness.** On the 9:16 file it measures the integrated loudness of the cold-open window and of the closing window, using FFmpeg's `loudnorm` with `print_format=json` and reading `input_i` (the bundled build has no `volumedetect`). The cold open must be at least 3 LU louder, because it carries the answer's own sound and the closing has only music. The report gives both measured values, and confirms that muting answers makes this check fail.
   - **Stills.** It also writes PNG stills (`renderStill` at frames taken from the manifest's segments) of the cold open, a bridge slot, a voice answer, a lower third and the closing, in both shapes, to `out/frames/`.
7. **CI.** The `render` job uploads both videos and `out/frames/` as the artifact `render-stills`.

**Tests.**
- The render package's tests cover any pure helper added: letterbox sizes for both shapes, and media fit.
- Story tests check the v2 `fixtureManifest`: `manifestErrors` is empty in both shapes.
- The CI render job runs as above.

## T-016d · The render input and the renderer port · needs T-014c, T-015c, T-016a

**Goal.** The server can build a checked render input for an episode from what is stored, start a render through a port once, follow it to the end, and record the file and its cost. Everything is tested with a fixture renderer.

**Do.**
1. **Contracts.** `packages/contracts/src/episode/render.ts` adds `EpisodeRender { id, episodeId, planVersion, renderVersion ≥ 1, format: 'portrait' | 'landscape', vendorRenderId?, bucket?, outKey, durationMs ≥ 1, state: 'starting' | 'rendering' | 'done' | 'failed', reason?, costMicroUsd?, startedAt, finishedAt? }`. `CostLedgerRow` gains step `render`, provider `remotionLambda` and unit `render`.
2. **Settings.** `pipeline/render/settings.ts` holds:
   - `PRESIGN_SECONDS = 900`;
   - `RENDER_COMPOSITION = 'Episode'`;
   - the render options: codec `h264`, image format `jpeg`, privacy `no-acl`, `maxRetries` 1, `timeoutInMilliseconds` 60000, log level `warn`, `deleteAfter` `1-day`;
   - `MAX_INPUT_BYTES = 200_000`;
   - `PHOTO_TYPES = ['image/jpeg', 'image/png', 'image/webp']`.
3. **The port.** `Renderer` in `ports.ts`:
   - `start({ manifest, composition, outKey, options }) → { renderId, bucketName }`;
   - `progress({ renderId, bucketName }) → { state: 'rendering', fraction } | { state: 'done', costUsd?, bytes? } | { state: 'failed', reason }`, where `reason` is one of a few fixed short texts (`render failed`, `render timed out`) and never a vendor message.

   `providers/fixture/renderer.ts` stores each request. On `start` it puts a small MP4 (a byte constant in its own file) at `outKey` in R2. `progress` reports `done` after a number of calls a test can set (default 1), or what a test sets (`failed`). It is reset with the other fixtures. `pipelineProviders` returns it only with `PROVIDERS: 'fixture'`; the real one arrives in T-016e.
4. **URLs.**
   - `providers/r2/presign.ts` exports `presignGet(env, key, expiresInSeconds) → Promise<string>`. It only signs, with `aws4fetch`, against `https://{R2_ACCOUNT_ID}.r2.cloudflarestorage.com/{R2_BUCKET}/{key}`.
   - `pipeline/render/mediaUrl.ts` exports `mediaUrl(env, owner: { userId, documentaryId }, key)`. It throws unless `key` starts with `u/{userId}/{documentaryId}/`, where the user is the documentary's `owner_user_id`, then calls `presignGet` with `PRESIGN_SECONDS`.
   - `R2_BUCKET` (`life-media`) goes in `wrangler.jsonc` `vars`; run `pnpm types:api` and then `pnpm format`.
   - `Env` gains `R2_ACCOUNT_ID?`, `R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID?` and `R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY?` (Worker secrets). Without them the first call throws `Rendering is not set up`.
   - `apps/api/vitest.config.ts` binds fixed test values. `aws4fetch` joins the boundary rule's vendor list.
5. **Data.**
   - The next D1 migration adds `renders` (the `EpisodeRender` fields), with its migration test. `episode_id` references `episodes` with ON DELETE CASCADE, and `(episode_id, render_version, format)` is unique.
   - `data/repositories/renders.ts` has `put`, `get`, `forEpisode` and `setState`, each parsing with the contract.
   - `episodes.ts` gains `setRender(episodeId, renderVersion, mp4Key, durationMs)`, which only moves `renderVersion` forward.
   - `mediaKeys.ts` gains `episodeKey(userId, documentaryId, episodeId, renderVersion, format)`.
   - `renderMicroUsd(costUsd)` = ⌈costUsd × 1,000,000⌉.
6. **Step functions** in `pipeline/render/*.ts`, plain functions over `{ db, media, renderer, env, clock }`:
   - `renderInput(episode, format) → RenderManifestV2 | null`:
     - it reads the current stored plan, and rebuilds the brief (`briefInput` + `weekBrief`), so `leavesDevice` runs again;
     - plan moments not in the brief are dropped;
     - for each remaining media moment, the original must exist in R2 (`head` of `mediaKey(…, 'original')`), and a photo must have a `PHOTO_TYPES` content type, else the moment is dropped;
     - `media.type` comes from the asset's kind (`audio` → `voice`);
     - answers get `answerWords` from their `workersAi` derived row;
     - narration is the kept clips of the plan version, and music is none (the catalogue is empty);
     - every `src` goes through `mediaUrl`;
     - it returns `episodeTimeline(…)`.
   - `startRender(episode, format)`:
     - A row for this episode, plan version and format in `rendering` or `done` is returned as it is, with no call.
     - A `null` input → `{ outcome: 'nothing' }`, and so does a landscape export while `renderVersion` is 0.
     - Otherwise it picks the render version: for portrait, 1 + the highest portrait `renderVersion` in the episode's rows; for landscape, the episode's `renderVersion`. It then writes a `starting` row with `episodeKey` and the manifest's `durationMs`, calls `renderer.start`, and updates the row to `rendering` with the vendor ids → `{ outcome: 'started', id }`.
     - A `starting` row left by a failed attempt is started again.
   - `checkRender(id)`:
     - `rendering` → `{ outcome: 'rendering' }`;
     - `done` → the row `done` with its `costMicroUsd`; for portrait, `setRender` with the row's `durationMs`; the episode's `render` ledger row rewritten as the sum over its rows (units = how many have a cost), then `costCents` → `{ outcome: 'done' }`;
     - `failed` → the row `failed` with its reason → `{ outcome: 'failed' }`.
     - A finished row returns its outcome without calling the renderer.

**Tests** (Workers pool, D1 and R2 in Miniflare, fixture renderer, fixed test R2 credentials):
- A narrated episode with originals in R2 builds a manifest whose sources are presigned URLs for the right keys (`X-Amz-Expires=900`, with the host and path checked), and whose narration and person speech never overlap.
- **Dropped moments:**
  - a moment whose original is missing is dropped;
  - a photo original stored as `image/heic` is dropped and gets no URL;
  - a moment made `localOnly` after planning (seeded straight into D1) is not in the manifest, and no URL is made for it.
- An episode with no originals gives `nothing`.
- **Portrait renders and cost:**
  - `startRender` then `checkRender` ends `done`, with the MP4 at `u/…/episodes/{id}/v1.mp4`, the episode's `renderVersion` 1 and `mp4Key` and `durationMs` (from the row) set, one ledger row and `costCents` updated;
  - `startRender` twice for the same plan version starts one render;
  - a render of plan version 2 makes `v2`, and the ledger row then has units 2 and the summed µUSD;
  - a failed render leaves the episode unchanged, and the next `startRender` starts a new one.
- **Landscape export:** it writes `v1-wide.mp4` and leaves `renderVersion` at 1; before any portrait render it gives `nothing`.
- **`mediaUrl`** refuses a key from another user or documentary, and `presignGet` refuses to run without credentials.
- No manifest or URL is logged.

**Acceptance.** API, contracts and story typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and `deploy:dry` pass. UNVERIFIED until the owner's accounts: a presigned URL read from outside Cloudflare.

## T-016e · Remotion Lambda · needs T-016b, T-016d

**Goal.** Real renders run on the owner's Remotion Lambda from the Worker, the site and function can be deployed with one command, and the payloads are proven to match Remotion's own.

**Do.**
1. **The provider**, in `apps/api/src/providers/remotionLambda/`:
   - `payload.ts` has pure builders for the `start` and `status` payloads, modelled on `remotion-lambda` 4.0.529 (`pip download remotion-lambda==4.0.529`; read `remotionclient.py` and `models.py`). They map the options they are given (from T-016d's settings) into Remotion's shape:
     - `outName` = `{ bucketName: R2_BUCKET, key: outKey, s3OutputProvider: { endpoint: https://{R2_ACCOUNT_ID}.r2.cloudflarestorage.com, accessKeyId, secretAccessKey } }`, and the status payload carries the same `s3OutputProvider`;
     - input props go inline and are refused above `MAX_INPUT_BYTES`;
     - the Remotion version constant is `4.0.529`.
   - `renderer.ts` implements `Renderer` with `aws4fetch` (`service: 'lambda'`, `retries: 0`), as a synchronous invoke of `REMOTION_FUNCTION_NAME` in `REMOTION_REGION`.
     - It parses the response like the Python client: it splits the body into JSON objects and takes the last.
     - `errorMessage`, `type: 'error'` or an `X-Amz-Function-Error` header throws a fixed short text, never a key, a URL or a vendor message.
     - Progress maps `done`, `fatalErrorEncountered`/`errors` (to the fixed reasons), `overallProgress`, `costs.accruedSoFar` and `outputSizeInBytes`, and ignores unknown fields.
   - `Env` gains `REMOTION_AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID?`, `REMOTION_AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY?`, `REMOTION_REGION?`, `REMOTION_FUNCTION_NAME?` and `REMOTION_SERVE_URL?`. All are Worker secrets set with `wrangler secret put`, since dashboard variables are wiped by the next deploy. Without them the first call throws `Rendering is not set up`.
   - `pipelineProviders` returns this renderer unless `PROVIDERS: 'fixture'`.
2. **A Node test project** in `apps/api/test-node/`, with `vitest.node.config.ts` and its own tsconfig (Node types, included in lint). The Workers config excludes it, and `pnpm test` runs both. `@remotion/lambda-client` 4.0.529 is a dev dependency. The tests check:
   - our start payload equals Remotion's own, built with `LambdaClientInternals.renderMediaOnLambdaOptionalToRequired` and `makeLambdaRenderMediaPayload` for the same options, field by field, ignoring only fields Remotion fills at random (name them in the report);
   - our status payload equals Remotion's status payload for the same render, if the package exports a builder for it (name it); else the fields match `models.py`'s `RenderProgressParams`;
   - our version constant equals the `version` in Remotion's payload and in `@remotion/lambda-client/package.json`.
3. **The deploy script.** `packages/render/scripts/lambda-deploy.mts` (script `lambda:deploy`; `@remotion/lambda` 4.0.529 as a dev dependency) runs in `REMOTION_REGION`:
   - `getOrCreateBucket({ region, enableFolderExpiry: true })`;
   - `deploySite` into that bucket (site `life-episode`);
   - `deployFunction` with 2048 MB memory, 2048 MB disk, a 240 s timeout, and a log group kept 7 days.

   It then prints the function name and serve URL to set as Worker secrets. It reads AWS credentials from the environment only.
4. **`docs/ops/RENDER.md`**: the owner's steps, in plain words:
   - an AWS account and a region (EU or US; the choice decides where rendering happens, and the privacy page names AWS as a processor);
   - the deploy user and role from `npx remotion lambda policies user|role`;
   - a second IAM user for the Worker, allowed only `lambda:InvokeFunction` on `remotion-render-*`, with its keys set as Worker secrets;
   - an R2 API token with Object Read and Write on the media bucket only, set as Worker secrets with the account id;
   - the Lambda concurrency check (`npx remotion lambda quotas`), with an increase requested if needed;

**Tests.**
