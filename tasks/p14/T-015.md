# T-015 · P14 Narration

Text for T-015a–c. Published 2026-09-27; this file does not change after publication. Fix lists are written in `tasks/TASKS.md` under the task.

## Preamble

**Goal of P14.** After planning (P13), the pipeline turns each narrator line of a model plan (the scene bridges and the tease) into speech with ElevenLabs Flash, stores each clip in R2 with its measured length and word timings, gives every answer its word timings from the transcript, keeps the narrator under 25% of spoken time using the measured lengths, and leaves the episode ready to render. Placing clips and captions on the final episode timeline is P15's (the renderer owns what the episode looks like, ARCHITECTURE §6). As in P12 and P13, the vendor sits behind a port and everything is built and tested with a fixture narrator; the real calls need the owner's ElevenLabs account.

**Read first:** `CLAUDE.md` (rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14), `docs/VISION.md` line 28 and §5, `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 "Narration" and "Render", §6, §7, DECISIONS D3, D12, D20, D38, D39, D40, `tasks/p13/T-014.md` and its notes in `tasks/TASKS.md` (the plan this starts from), `tasks/p12/T-013.md` (ports, providers, the ledger).

**Decided here (DECISIONS D40).**
- **What is spoken.** Only the plan's scene bridges and its tease, in plan order (`narrationLines`). A recap plan has none and skips this step. The person's answers are never synthesised or re-voiced (D3, D12; no cloning).
- **The cast.** Our ids `narrator-1` … `narrator-4` map to four ElevenLabs voice ids in `NARRATOR_VOICES` (a JSON object), set by the owner with `wrangler secret put` after choosing and listening (a dashboard variable would be wiped by the next deploy), never in code. ElevenLabs' Default voices expire on 31 December 2026, so the owner picks from voices that stay available (the named replacement voices or voices the account owns). Choosing a narrator per person is later work; every plan uses the voice `assemblePlan` set (`narrator-1`).
- **The request.** `POST https://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/text-to-speech/{voiceId}/with-timestamps?output_format=mp3_44100_128`, header `xi-api-key`, body `{ text, model_id: 'eleven_flash_v2_5', language_code: 'en' }`, voice settings left at their defaults. Raw `fetch` from the provider, no SDK (the SDK brings Node-only dependencies). Responses are parsed leniently (unknown fields ignored, as ElevenLabs' API terms require), and errors are handled by HTTP status.
- **Length and word timings come from the alignment.** The response's `alignment` (characters with start and end seconds) gives each clip's length (the last character's end) and its word timings (each word from its first character's start to its last character's end). Captions are word-level (DESIGN §4), and a clip cannot be made again with the same timing, so the words are stored, never only chunks; P15 groups them into caption pages. A line whose response has no alignment, or that still fails after the step's retries, is left out (missing stays missing, rule 10); the rest of the episode goes on.
- **The 25% cap on real audio.** After synthesis, `fitNarration` measures the narrator's share with the real clip lengths against the person's spoken time, taken from `narratorShare(plan, brief)` (`spokenMs − narratorMs`, the one owner of that sum). While the share is above 25%, it drops the tease first, then bridges from the last scene backwards. Dropped lines keep their stored clips (a later edit may use them) but are marked not kept.
- **Cache.** A clip is keyed by a SHA-256 of (vendor voice id, model id, output format, text). A retry, a re-run or a later plan version reuses a stored clip with the same key instead of paying again. Audio is never regenerated expecting the same bytes (ElevenLabs does not promise determinism).
- **Storage.** Clips live at `u/{userId}/{documentaryId}/narration/{episodeId}/{hash}.mp3` (under `u/`, so the `tmp/` lifecycle rule never touches them; deleted with the account in P21). Rows in D1 `narration_clips`: episode, plan version, line index, line kind (`bridge` or `tease`), scene index, text, our voice id, key, hash, length, cues, kept, created at.
- **Answer timings come from P12.** Whisper's segments, with their words, are stored with the transcript (the T-013b–d fix lists); the phone does not store them. `answerWords` uses the words relative to the answer's start, or each segment's words spread over the segment when Whisper gave no words, or the transcript's words spread evenly over the answer when there are no segments; the last two are marked approximate (the words are real, only their timing is estimated).
- **Numbers.** Flash reads digits badly and ElevenLabs only normalises text on Enterprise plans, so the planner writes numbers, dates and times in words (a note on T-014b), `planVoiceErrors` flags a digit in a bridge or the tease so P13's retry fixes it (a note on T-014a), and `speakable(text)` still refuses a line with a digit as the last check (logged by index, never by text).
- **Costs.** One ledger row per episode for step `narrate`, provider `elevenlabs`, unit `character`: the characters of every call ElevenLabs answered, spoken or not (the `character-cost` response header, or the text length when it is missing), at 50 µUSD per character ($0.05 per 1,000). A reused or skipped line costs nothing, and a run that sent nothing writes no row (so it never erases an earlier run's cost).
- **Privacy.** The person's own sentences never reach ElevenLabs (P13's copy check), but details of the week do (names, places, events in the bridges). So before the real narrator is turned on, the owner turns off "Improve the models for everyone" in the ElevenLabs account and confirms no training in its terms (VISION §8); until then only the fixture narrator runs.

**Stack (checked 2026-09-27).**
- ElevenLabs: `eleven_flash_v2_5` (32 languages, 40,000 characters per request, about 75 ms); with-timestamps returns `{ audio_base64, alignment: { characters, character_start_times_seconds, character_end_times_seconds } | null, normalized_alignment: same | null }`; response headers `request-id` and `character-cost`; errors 400/401/402/403/404/409/422/429/5xx (429 when a concurrency limit is reached: back off, never retry at once); `output_format` is a query parameter; `enable_logging=false` (zero retention) is Enterprise only. Whether Flash returns an alignment on with-timestamps is not documented: the owner's first real call checks it.
- The Workers test pool and Workflows as in P12/P13 (`remoteBindings: false`; `retries` needs `limit` and `delay`, and takes `backoff: 'exponential'`; `dispose()` introspectors in `finally`). A Worker cannot read a checked-in `.mp3` at run time (it is not a default module rule), so the fixture narrator's audio is a byte constant in its own file.
- `crypto.subtle.digest('SHA-256', …)` is available in Workers for the cache key.

**Rules for every P14 task.** Only plan text written by the planner (bridges and the tease, already checked by P13 for the voice rules and for copying the person's words) is ever sent to ElevenLabs; a test proves no transcript, note or caption text appears in a narrator request. Missing stays missing: a line with no audio or no alignment is left out, never replaced. The provider holds no product policy: the model id, the format, the cast mapping, the cap, the cache and the order of dropping live in `pipeline/`, `policy/` and `packages/story`. No test calls ElevenLabs or logs a key. Names avoid the banned-identifier list (for example no `speakerId`).

**Out of scope.** Placing clips and captions on the episode timeline, caption pages and the caption track (P15), choosing a narrator per person, voice cloning (never in release 1), streaming speech, request stitching, pronunciation dictionaries, other languages.

## T-015a · Narration shapes and rules · needs T-014a

**Goal.** The pure parts of narration exist and are tested: which lines are spoken, when a line may be spoken, how word timings come from an alignment or a transcript, and how the 25% cap is kept with measured lengths.

**Do.**
1. `packages/contracts/src/episode/narration.ts`: `NarrationLine { index, kind: 'bridge' | 'tease', sceneIndex?, text }`; `TimedWord { text 1..60, fromMs: int ≥ 0, toMs: int }` (`toMs` > `fromMs`); `NarrationClip { episodeId, planVersion, index, kind, sceneIndex?, text, voiceId, key, hash, durationMs: int ≥ 1, words: TimedWord[] (1..60), kept: boolean, createdAt }`. `CostLedgerRow` gains step `narrate`, provider `elevenlabs`, unit `character` (after T-013b's fix adds P13's values).
2. `narrationLines(plan)` → the bridges in scene order, then the tease, indexed from 0; none for a plan without narration.
3. `speakable(text)` → `true` unless the text has a digit or is empty after trimming.
4. `lineWords(text, alignment)` → the words of a line from a character alignment (`characters`, `startSeconds`, `endSeconds`, equal lengths): split on spaces, each word from its first character's start to its last character's end, in whole milliseconds; `null` when the alignment is missing, empty, of unequal lengths or does not spell the text.
5. `answerWords(derived, durationMs)` → `{ words: TimedWord[], approximate: boolean }` for an answer: the transcript segments' `words` when they have them; else each segment's words spread over that segment; else the transcript's words spread evenly over `durationMs` (the last two `approximate: true`); every time clamped to the answer's length, empty words dropped, no transcript → no words.
6. `fitNarration(lines, clipMs, plan, brief)` → the indices to keep: the person's spoken time is `narratorShare(plan, brief).spokenMs − narratorShare(plan, brief).narratorMs` (T-014a); while (Σ kept clip ms) × 4 > (Σ kept clip ms + person's spoken time), drop the tease first, then bridges from the last scene backwards.

**Tests.** `narrationLines` on a plan with three bridges and a tease; `speakable` refuses "Week 12" and passes "Twelve weeks in"; `lineWords` on a hand-made alignment gives each word's times, and `null` for a mismatched alignment; `answerWords` with word timings (exact), with segments but no words (approximate, inside each segment), and with neither (approximate, the last word ending at the answer's length); `fitNarration` keeps everything under the cap, drops the tease first, then the last scene's bridge, and keeps nothing when the person never speaks; `NarrationClip` refuses a word that ends before it starts and 61 words.

**Acceptance.** Contracts and story typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries pass; `packages/story` still imports only `@life/contracts` and `zod`.

## T-015b · The ElevenLabs provider · needs T-013c, T-015a

**Goal.** The pipeline can turn a line into speech through a port, with real and fixture adapters, and no test can reach ElevenLabs.

**Do.**
1. `Narrator` in `ports.ts`: `speak({ voiceId, text, modelId, outputFormat, languageCode }) → { audio: Uint8Array, alignment: { characters: string[], startSeconds: number[], endSeconds: number[] } | null, characters: number }`. The pipeline gets the vendor voice id from `vendorVoice` (Do 5) and passes it in.
2. `providers/elevenlabs/narrator.ts`: the request from "Decided here" with the values it is given; decodes `audio_base64`; maps `alignment` (not `normalized_alignment`) or `null`; `characters` from the `character-cost` header, else the text length; parses the body with a non-strict schema; throws on a non-2xx status with the status in the message and never the key or the text. Without `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` its first call throws `Narration is not set up`.
3. `providers/fixture/narrator.ts`: records calls and returns a small valid MP3 (one silent MPEG frame, as a byte constant in the file) with an alignment spelling the text at a pace a test can set (default 60 ms per character), or what a test sets (no alignment, an error); `providers/fixture/index.ts` resets it with the others.
4. `pipelineProviders(env)` also returns the narrator (fixture only with `PROVIDERS: 'fixture'`). `Env` gains `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY?` and `NARRATOR_VOICES?` (both Worker secrets, set by the owner); `apps/api/vitest.config.ts` binds a fixture `NARRATOR_VOICES`.
5. `pipeline/narrate/settings.ts`: `NARRATE_MODEL = 'eleven_flash_v2_5'`, `NARRATE_FORMAT = 'mp3_44100_128'`, `NARRATE_LANGUAGE = 'en'`; `vendorVoice(env, ourId)` parses `NARRATOR_VOICES` (a JSON object of `narrator-1`…`narrator-4` to non-empty strings) and throws `The narrator voices are not set up` when it is missing, not JSON, or lacks the id.
6. `narrateMicroUsd(characters)` in `policy/costs.ts` = 50 × characters.

**Tests.** The adapter against a stubbed `fetch`: the URL with the voice id and `output_format` query, the `xi-api-key` header, the body with text, model id and language code; it maps the alignment, returns `null` alignment when the response has none, reads `character-cost`, ignores unknown response fields, and throws on 429 with the status and without the text or key in the message; without a key it throws `Narration is not set up`; `vendorVoice` for a good map, a missing variable, bad JSON and a missing id; `narrateMicroUsd(140)` is 7,000; the boundary check passes (no vendor SDK).

**Acceptance.** API typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries, `deploy:dry` pass. UNVERIFIED until the owner's account: a real call, whether Flash returns an alignment, the chosen voices.

## T-015c · EpisodePipeline step 3: narrate · needs T-014c, T-015a, T-015b

**Goal.** An episode whose plan has narration gets a clip for each line it can speak, the cap is kept on real lengths, the clips and their cues are stored, the cost is recorded, and the episode moves on to rendering.

**Do.**
1. The next D1 migration (from `schema.ts`): `narration_clips` (the `NarrationClip` fields, `words` as JSON text; `episode_id` references `episodes` with ON DELETE CASCADE; unique `(episode_id, plan_version, index)`), with its migration test. `data/repositories/narration.ts`: `put`, `forEpisode(episodeId, planVersion)`, `byHash(episodeId, hash)`, `setKept(episodeId, planVersion, indices)`. `mediaKeys.ts` gains `narrationKey(userId, documentaryId, episodeId, hash)`, where the user is the documentary's `owner_user_id`.
2. `pipeline/narrate/*.ts`, plain functions over `{ db, media, narrator, env, clock }`, each reading the stored plan (and, for the fit, the brief through T-014c's `briefInput` and `weekBrief`) from D1 itself: `linesStep(episode)` → `{ planVersion, indices }` (empty unless the episode's state is `narrating`); `lineStep(episode, planVersion, index)`: when `speakable` refuses the line, return `{ outcome: 'skipped' }`; the hash; a stored clip with that hash for this episode is copied to this plan version's row and reused (`{ outcome: 'reused' }`); otherwise `speak`, `lineWords` (a `null` result means `{ outcome: 'no-alignment', characters }` and nothing stored), put the MP3 in R2 (content type `audio/mpeg`) at `narrationKey`, store the row with `durationMs` = the last character's end and `kept: true`; return `{ outcome: 'spoken', characters }`. `fitStep(episode, planVersion)`: `fitNarration` over the stored clips of this plan version; `setKept`; state `rendering`.
3. `EpisodePipeline.ts`, after the plan steps: `narrate-lines`; one `narrate-{index}` step per index (`retries: { limit: 5, delay: '30 seconds', backoff: 'exponential' }`, because a 429 is common when a time-zone bucket starts together; `run` catches a step that still fails and goes on without that line); then `narrate-fit`; then `narrate-costs` (one ledger upsert for `narrate` / `character` over the characters every answered call returned, by `narrateMicroUsd`, skipped when that is 0, then `costCents`). Step results carry outcomes, indices and counts only, never audio or text.

**Tests** (Workers pool, D1 and R2 in Miniflare, the fixture narrator). A plan with three bridges and a tease stores four clips with their words and lengths, puts four MP3s in R2 under `u/…/narration/`, and ends in `rendering`; calling `lineStep` again for every line reuses every clip (no narrator call); a line with a digit is skipped without a call; a line whose response has no alignment is left out, the others kept, and its characters still counted; a narrator that fails every attempt for one line leaves that line out and the run completes; with the fixture set to a slow pace (for example 150 ms per character), a plan whose measured narration is over 25% ends with the tease and then the last bridge not kept; a recap plan has no narrate steps; no narrator request carries a transcript, a note or a caption from the week (seeded in D1); the ledger row counts the characters of every answered call, and a run that sent nothing writes no row.
Allowed test changes: T-014c's Workflow tests that expect the run to end in `narrating` now expect `rendering`.

**Acceptance.** API, contracts and story typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries, `deploy:dry` pass. UNVERIFIED until the owner's account: real speech for a real plan; training turned off and confirmed in ElevenLabs' terms before the real narrator is turned on.
