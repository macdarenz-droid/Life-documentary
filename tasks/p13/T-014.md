# T-014 · P13 Planner

Text for T-014a–d. Published 2026-09-27; this file does not change after publication. Fix lists are written in `tasks/TASKS.md` under the task.

## Preamble

**Goal of P13.** After understanding (P12), the pipeline turns the week into an episode plan: Claude Sonnet 5 reads the week brief and picks and orders the week's moments into scenes, writes the title, headings, bridges, tease and summary, and picks the music mood, under a fixed, cached style prompt. The server builds everything timed (shot lengths, the cold open's cut, Ken Burns, lower-third times, the duration) from the moments themselves, checks the plan with `validatePlan` and the voice rules, retries once with the first answer and its errors, and falls back to the recap plan so an episode always arrives (D20). The plan is stored, its summary feeds later briefs, and an evaluation runner scores the planner over the 20 fixture weeks. As in P12, model calls go through a port and everything is built and tested with a fixture planner; the real calls need the owner's Anthropic key.

**Read first:** `CLAUDE.md` (rules 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14), `docs/VISION.md` §5 and line 28 ("it never paraphrases the user"), `docs/design/DESIGN.md` §2 (Ken Burns) and §8, `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 "Episode plan", §5 "Episode", §6, §7, §9, DECISIONS D3, D10, D12, D20, D38, D39, `tasks/p12/T-013.md` (the pipeline, ports and providers this extends) and `tasks/p11/T-006.md` (the plan contract, `validatePlan`, `recapPlan`).

**Decided here (DECISIONS D39).**
- **The model chooses, the server times.** The brief has no word timings, so the model cannot know where to cut an answer (rule 10). `PlannerOutput` (below) names moments only: which answer opens cold, which moments go in which scene in which order, which closes, who gets a lower third. The server makes every shot from its moment, with the recap's own rules shared in one function: an answer plays whole, a clip plays its first 6 s at most, a photo holds 3 s with the design's Ken Burns (1.0 to 1.06). The cold open plays its answer whole. A lower third appears 0.5 s into the first shot of its moment, and is dropped when that moment has no shot. The server also adds `version`, `episodeNumber`, `weekStart`, `weekEnd`, `targetDurationMs`, `captionsFromTranscript: true` on every scene (rule 14) and `narratorVoiceId`.
- **Length.** A model plan runs from the cold open through the title card (3 s), the scenes and the closing shot (2.5 s): at least 30 s and at most 240 s. Answers are at most 10 s, so T-006's 60 s floor would push most real weeks to the recap; the model-mode floor in `validatePlan` becomes 30 s. The model plan's total is not clamped; `recapPlan` keeps its own total exactly as it is.
- **Who gets a model plan.** `planEligibility(brief)`: at least one answer, at least three media moments (a scene needs a shot), and a full-length total (cold open on the longest answer, every media moment as a shot, title and closing) of at least 30 s. Eligible weeks get a model plan; a week with media that is not eligible gets the recap without a model call; a week with no answer, clip or photo has no episode, and its episode row is removed (quiet weeks are not failures, D2).
- **Narrator voice.** Our own ids `narrator-1` … `narrator-4` (P14 maps them to vendor voices and lets the person choose). Until then every plan with a bridge or a tease gets `narrator-1`, set by the server, never by the model.
- **Checks.** In this order, the first that applies makes an answer unusable: `stop_reason` `refusal` (refused: no retry, straight to the recap); `max_tokens` or `model_context_window_exceeded` (`The answer was cut off.`); no text (`The answer had no text.`); text that is not JSON (`The answer was not JSON.`); `PlannerOutput` does not parse (each issue as `<path>: <code>`); `assemblePlan` fails (`<path>: <code>`); the total outside 30–240 s (`The episode is <n> s long, it must be 30 to 240 s.`); `validatePlan(…, 'model')` errors; `planVoiceErrors`; `planCopyErrors` (a bridge or the tease repeats five or more words in a row from a transcript, so the narrator would speak the person's words, VISION line 28). Error strings never quote model or user text.
- **One retry, in the same step.** An unusable (not refused) first answer gets one more call in the same step, as a conversation: the brief, the first answer as the assistant turn, then the errors. A second unusable answer, a refusal or a step that still fails after its retry gives the recap plan. At most two answered calls per episode. Nothing but outcomes, error strings and token counts leaves the step (Workflow state is kept for 30 days); the plan is stored in D1 by the step itself.
- **Storage.** Plans are rows (`episode_plans`: episode, version, the plan JSON, `createdBy` `model` or `recap`, created at; deleted with their episode). The episode's `planVersion` points at the one in use and its `summary` is that plan's summary. Later briefs read the summaries of the last three earlier episodes whose plan is a model plan, oldest first (recap summaries carry no story). Storyline summaries (T-008's mention) are later work.
- **Request.** `claude-sonnet-5` (one constant), adaptive thinking (the default, left on), effort `medium` (the eval compares `medium` and `high` before it is changed), `max_tokens` 16000 (below the SDK's non-streaming limit of about 21,333), the style prompt as one system block with `cache_control: { type: 'ephemeral', ttl: '1h' }` (Sunday runs of one time-zone bucket fall inside an hour), the brief JSON as the first user message, and `output_config.format` built from `PlannerOutput`. No user data in the system block (caches are shared across users in the workspace). No temperature, no prefill (both are 400 on Sonnet 5).
- **Costs.** One ledger row per episode, step `plan` and unit: each row's µUSD is its unit price times that unit's count summed over the calls, rounded up once per row. Prices: input 2, output 10, 1-hour cache write 4, cache read 0.2 µUSD per token (thinking is billed as output). T-013b's note already widens the ledger for this.

**Stack (checked 2026-09-27).**
- Sonnet 5: model id `claude-sonnet-5` (dateless, pinned); $2 / $10 per MTok; cache write 1h $4, read $0.20; 1M context; 128K output. Structured outputs are GA: `output_config: { format: { type: 'json_schema', schema } }`. Schema limits: `additionalProperties: false` on every object, `minItems` only 0 or 1, no `minLength`/`maxLength`/`minimum`/`maximum`/`maxItems`; at most 24 optional properties and 16 union-typed properties per request; a schema that is too complex is a 400. Output can still miss the schema on `refusal`, `max_tokens` or `model_context_window_exceeded`, and enum casing is not guaranteed.
- `@anthropic-ai/sdk` 0.128.0 (P12's pin): `zodOutputFormat(schema)` from `@anthropic-ai/sdk/helpers/zod` needs a Zod 4 schema and moves every keyword the API does not support (including `enum`, `const`, lengths and bounds) into the description text, so only the object shape is enforced by the API and our own Zod parse checks the rest. Use `client.messages.create({ …, output_config: { format: zodOutputFormat(PlannerOutput) } })`, not `messages.parse` (it throws before the caller can read `stop_reason`). `usage` has `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`.
- Prompt caching: the minimum cached prefix on Sonnet 5 is 1,024 tokens; a shorter one silently does not cache. The cache key covers the format, the system block, thinking and effort settings: any byte change there starts a new cache.
- Workflows (as in P12): `retries` needs both `limit` and `delay` in the installed types (`{ limit: 1, delay: '10 seconds' }`); a step without a config gets 5 retries by default; introspection with `dispose()` in `finally`; `mockStepError({ name }, error, 1)` fails a step once.
- The eval runner runs in Node with its own Vitest config, never in the Workers pool; Vitest refuses unknown command-line flags, so options come from environment variables.

**`PlannerOutput`** (`packages/contracts/src/episode/plannerOutput.ts`; reuse the music mood schema of `plan.ts` by export, no copies):
```
title: string 1..60
subtitle?: string 1..80
coldOpen: { momentId }                                          an answer; it plays whole
scenes: 3..5 × { heading 1..60, storylineId?, shots: 1..6 × { momentId }, narratorBridge?: { text 1..140 } }
closing: { momentId }
tease?: { storylineId, text 1..100 }
musicMood: MusicMood
lowerThirds: 0..20 × { momentId, castId }
summary: string 1..400
```

**The style prompt** (`apps/api/src/pipeline/plan/stylePrompt.ts`, exported as one constant, sent unchanged as the system block):

> You plan one episode of a personal documentary. The person records a short answer to one question each day and adds photos, clips and notes. Once a week you turn their week into an episode of up to four minutes. Their own recorded answers are the heart of it. You arrange what they recorded. You never put words in their mouth.
>
> You get the week as JSON: the moments in order, each with its day and weekday, its kind (answer, clip, photo or note), its length, the question it answers, a transcript of what they said when there is one, a plain description of the picture when there is one, their own note, their mood if they set one, and the storylines and people they tagged. You also get the summaries of up to three earlier episodes, so the story can carry on from where it was.
>
> Your answer is the plan, in the shape you're given. You choose moments and put them in order. Every moment you use must be one from this week's list, named by its id. Use only what is there. Don't invent places, people, times or events, and don't fill a gap with a guess.
>
> How an episode goes. It opens cold on one answer, the strongest line of the week, and that must be an answer. Then the title. Then three to five scenes, grouped by storyline where the week has one, otherwise by day, in the order things happened. Each scene has a short heading and one to six shots, each shot one moment. Notes have no picture or sound, so they are never shots, but they can tell you what a day was about. The episode ends on a closing shot that lets the week settle. Add a tease of next week only when a storyline is still open, in one line. Add a lower third only for a person the person tagged in that moment, by their cast id, and only on a moment you use as a shot.
>
> How long it runs. You don't set times. Each answer plays whole, a clip plays up to six seconds, a photo holds three seconds, the title card three and the closing two and a half. The cold open, the title card, every shot and the closing together must come to at least 30 seconds and no more than four minutes. Choose enough moments to reach that honestly, and when the week has more than fits, leave out the weaker ones.
>
> Storylines. When a storyline runs through the week, give it a scene of its own and put its id on that scene. When an earlier summary mentions a storyline that moved this week, a bridge may say where it stands now. When nothing moved, leave it out.
>
> The narrator. The person's own voice carries the episode. The narrator only links scenes with a short bridge, at most one per scene, and says the tease. Everything the narrator says, taken together, must stay well under a quarter of the time anyone is speaking in the episode. When in doubt, leave the bridge out. A bridge frames the scene: the day, the place, where a storyline stands, using only what the week shows. It never retells what the person said, never quotes them, never sums them up and never guesses how they felt. Their words stay theirs. Good bridges: "Tuesday. The plumber still hasn't come." "Saturday, at the allotment." "Back at the new job."
>
> Words. Everything you write is read or heard by the person, so it has to sound like a careful person wrote it. Plain, calm and specific. Short words. Contractions where you'd say them. Sentence case for the title and the headings: "The week the kitchen flooded", not "The Week The Kitchen Flooded". British spelling. No exclamation marks, no dashes of any kind between words, no semicolons and no emoji. No stock phrases and nothing that sounds like an advert, a coach or a chatbot: no "journey", "unforgettable", "a week to remember", "moments that matter", "at a glance", "embrace", "unlock", "cherish" or "vibes". Don't write a poetic line where a plain one works. No guilt and no games: never mention missed days, streaks, scores or goals. Never say or suggest that anything recognised a face or a voice. People are named only by the names the person gave them, and places only by the names the person gave them.
>
> The title says what this week was about, in the person's own world: "The week the kitchen flooded", "Mum's first week home", "Rain, and the new bike". Never a generic title such as "My week", "This week", "Week 12", "A week in review", "Highlights" or "Weekly recap". The subtitle, when you add one, is a short plain line under it.
>
> The summary is two or three plain sentences about what happened this week, written for the next episodes to build on. It states only what the week shows, in the third person, with no judgement of how the week went.
>
> Music. Pick the one mood that fits the week as a whole: calm, warm, bright, bittersweet or driving.
>
> A thin week is fine. Make a short, honest episode from what there is. Don't pad it and don't invent anything to fill it.
>
> When your earlier answer and a list of its errors follow, answer again with the whole plan, changed only where it fixes every one of those errors.

## T-014d · The planner evaluation · needs T-014c

**Goal.** One command runs the real planner over the 20 fixture weeks and prints how each plan scores, so a prompt or model change can be judged before it ships.

**Do.**
1. `apps/api/src/pipeline/plan/evalReport.ts` (pure, runs in the Workers pool too): `evalRow(week, brief, result)` → the week's expectation (`planEligibility`), the outcome, each `planProperties` value, the tokens and µUSD; `passes(row)` (an eligible week passes when it got a model plan with every property true and `userVoiceShare` ≥ 0.75; a `recap` week passes with no model call; an `empty` week passes with nothing); `reportTable(rows)` → a Markdown table and a totals line (weeks passed, total µUSD).
2. `apps/api/eval/planner.eval.ts` with `apps/api/eval.config.ts` (Node environment) and `apps/api/eval/tsconfig.json` (Node types; add it to the lint setup so typed lint covers it), run by `pnpm --filter @life/api eval:planner`, never part of `pnpm test` or CI: for each fixture week (from `@life/story/fixtures`), `weekBrief` with episode number 1 and no earlier summaries, then, for eligible weeks, `planOnce` against the real planner (the retry included), then `evalRow`. Options from environment variables: `PLAN_EFFORT` (`low`, `medium`, `high`; default the settings' value) and `EVAL_WEEK` (one week number). It writes the table with `process.stdout.write`. Without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` it writes `Skipped: ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is not set.` and exits 0.
3. `docs/architecture/EVALS.md`: what the evaluation checks and why (the properties and the pass rule), how to run it, and a results table with one row "not run yet: needs the owner's key".

**Tests** (normal suite). `evalRow` and `passes` on hand-made results for each case (a passing model plan, one with a generic title, a recap week, an empty week); with the fixture planner answering a valid plan for every eligible week, the report has 20 rows, every row's expectation equals `planEligibility` of its brief, "photos only" and "clips only" expect the recap and "quiet week" and "notes only" expect nothing, and the totals line counts the passes.

**Acceptance.** API typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries pass; `eval:planner` without a key writes the skip line and exits 0. UNVERIFIED until the owner's key: a real run, recorded in `EVALS.md` by the supervisor.
