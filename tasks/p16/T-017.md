# T-017 · P16 Delivery

Text for T-017a–g. Published 2026-09-28. This file does not change after publication. Fix lists are written in `tasks/TASKS.md` under the task.

## Preamble

**Goal of P16.** Every week, on its own, each documentary gets its episode on the day and at the hour the person chose, and the phone tells them it is ready.
- A 15-minute cron starts each due week.
- `EpisodePipeline` runs understand → plan → narrate. It keeps the chosen answers, asks the phone for the full photos and clips the plan uses, waits for them until shortly before the hour, renders (P15) and publishes.
- The phone uploads what was asked for, receives "Episode N is ready", and plays the episode on an Episode screen, also offline.
- Each run records how long every stage took, so on-time delivery can be measured.

**Decided here (DECISIONS D42).**

- **Which week an episode covers.**
  - An episode delivered on date D (a day that falls on the documentary's `episodeDay`) covers the seven local days D−7 to D−1: `weekStart` = D−7, `weekEnd` = D−1.
  - With Sunday delivery the week runs Sunday to Saturday, and the delivery day's own moments go to next week's episode. Nothing is lost, nothing counts twice, and every day of the week is over before its run starts.
  - Moments are chosen by local day, as `weekBrief` already does.

- **When it runs.**
  - `deliverAt` is `episodeHour`:00 local on D.
  - `startAt` is 06:00 local on D. When `episodeHour` is before 07:00, `startAt` is instead the later of 00:00 on D and `deliverAt` − 60 minutes.
  - `renderAt` is the later of `startAt` and `deliverAt` − 15 minutes.
  - Six o'clock gives understanding up to two hours for caption batches (D38) and leaves the phone most of the day to send what the plan asks for.
  - A cron runs every 15 minutes (`*/15 * * * *`, UTC). Every real UTC offset ends in :00, :30 or :45.
  - The cron starts every week whose `startAt` has passed and whose `deliverAt` + 3 h has not, and whose episode does not exist yet. Documentaries whose owner has asked for their account to be deleted are skipped. It uses `createBatch` with the instance id `ep-{documentaryId}-{weekStart}` and passes the week's times as params.

- **The order of the run.**
  - The steps: understand → plan → keep the chosen answers → delete the working copies → narrate → ask for originals → wait → render → publish.
  - Working copies are now deleted after planning instead of at the end of understanding, because the chosen answers must be kept first. D38's lifecycle backstop still applies.
  - Only an empty week ends the run early, and it deletes the working copies first.
  - A recap plan, or a model plan with nothing to narrate, skips narration and goes on.
  - Narration runs before the wait because it needs no originals.

- **Answers need no second upload.**
  - An answer is uploaded as recorded (D37), so its working copy is the original.
  - For every answer the plan uses (cold open, shots or closing) that the rebuilt brief still has, the run copies `tmp/…/{asset}/answer` to `u/…/{asset}/original`, R2 to R2.
  - All working copies are then deleted as before. This matches VISION §8: only the full recordings chosen for the episode stay in the cloud.
  - When a moment is later deleted, its original is deleted with it.

- **Photos and clips are asked for.**
  - For each photo or clip the plan uses whose original is not already in R2, the run writes an `OriginalRequest` row. The phone learns of it through `/sync` (pull only).
  - `leavesDevice` gains `requested`, so an asked-for original may leave the phone without Cloud backup. This holds on both sides (rule 8).
  - The upload route accepts `original` only for an asset with an open request, or with Cloud backup.
  - Every photo original is re-encoded to JPEG on the phone, in the foreground only, since a full-size decode needs hundreds of MB. This also drops EXIF, including location. Video still waits for Wi-Fi (D37).
  - When the last open request of an episode is met, the upload route sends the Workflow event `originals-ready`.

- **Reaching the phone.**
  - Three routes, best first:
    1. The app is opened, which runs sync.
    2. A silent push when the requests are written. This is best effort: iOS allows two or three an hour, gives about 30 s and drops them after a force-quit.
    3. The background task from P6.
  - Nothing depends on any one of them. At `renderAt` the run renders with what arrived. An original that did not arrive leaves its shot out (rule 10, T-016d), and the person's answers still carry the episode.

- **Delivery.**
  - After a render, the run sleeps until `deliverAt` (not at all if that has passed), sets the episode `ready` with `deliveredAt`, and sends one visible push: title "Episode 12 is ready", no body, and data `{ episodeId }` only. The model-written title shows in the app after sync, never in the push.
  - If a step fails after its retries at or after planning, or the render fails or has nothing to render, the run stores the recap plan as the next plan version and renders that (D20).
  - If that also fails, the episode is `failed`: no push, the error logged, and a `failed` run record. The app says the episode couldn't be made. This replaces D20's dated delay, and a later task can add a retry.
  - An empty week ends quietly (D2). Its episode row is removed as in P13, and its run is still recorded.

- **Push.**
  - Pushes go through Expo's push service with plain `fetch` from a provider, no SDK, on the Android channel `episodes`.
  - Push data passes through Expo, Apple and Google, so it carries ids and our own fixed words only.
  - Tokens come from `getExpoPushTokenAsync({ projectId })` and are registered with the device. A token lives on one device row only.
  - `DeviceNotRegistered` clears a token.
  - An account deletion request clears the account's tokens, and nothing is pushed to such an account.
  - The optional `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` is a Worker secret.
  - With no project id (before the owner runs `eas init`) or no permission, no token is sent and nothing else changes.

- **Watching.**
  - An episode syncs down to the phone (pull only, without storage keys) once a plan is stored, so a week that ends empty never reaches the phone.
  - The phone works out where this week stands from its own clock, `episodeTimes` and the pulled summary:
    - "being made" from `startAt` until the episode is ready;
    - "running late" once `deliverAt` has passed and it is not ready;
    - "couldn't be made" when it failed;
    - no episode this week when `deliverAt` + 3 h has passed with no summary.
  - The video comes from `GET /episodes/:id/video`: session auth, the owner's documentary only, byte ranges (iOS needs them), `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. No R2 credentials are involved.
  - For offline viewing, the phone downloads the four newest ready episodes on Wi-Fi and encrypts each into its file store (D14), then plays through `decryptToCache` like the moment viewer. It deletes older copies and replaced render versions. `expo-video`'s own cache is not used, because it keeps only what was played and is keyed by URL.

- **Measuring.**
  - One `episode_runs` row per documentary and week (with the episode id when there is one) records `startedAt`, when each stage ended, `dueAt` (= `deliverAt`), `deliveredAt`, the outcome (`model` / `recap` / `empty` / `failed`) and the counts of originals asked for and received.
  - D1 rows are testable and queryable; Analytics Engine writes nothing locally.
  - The targets are VISION §9's: 95% by the hour, 99% within three hours.

**Stack (checked 2026-09-27).**

- **Cron Triggers.** `"triggers": { "crons": ["*/15 * * * *"] }` in wrangler config, and with Hono `export default { fetch: app.fetch, scheduled }`. Test by calling `scheduled` with `createScheduledController({ scheduledTime, cron })`, `createExecutionContext()` and `waitOnExecutionContext` from `cloudflare:test`.
- **Workflows.**
  - `step.waitForEvent(name, { type, timeout })` returns `{ payload, timestamp, type }` and throws on timeout (so wrap it in try/catch). Events sent before the wait are kept.
  - `instance.sendEvent({ type, payload })`.
  - `createBatch` takes at most 100 per call.
  - `step.sleepUntil` with a time in the past throws in the test pool, so only future times are passed.
  - Test pool helpers: `mockEvent({ type, payload })`, `forceEventTimeout({ name })` and `disableSleeps()`.
  - The local engine never reports `waiting`. Whether a second `createBatch` with the same id is skipped is not checked locally, so the cron test uses a stub binding.
- **Expo push.**
  - `POST https://exp.host/--/api/v2/push/send`, at most 100 messages per request, 4 KB payload.
  - Fields: `to`, `title`, `body`, `data`, `sound`, `priority`, `channelId`, `contentAvailable`.
  - Tickets come back in order, and `DeviceNotRegistered` means stop sending to that token.
  - A silent push is `data` + `contentAvailable: true` + `priority: 'normal'`.
  - Tokens look like `ExponentPushToken[…]` or `ExpoPushToken[…]`.
- **expo-notifications 57.0.21.**
  - `getExpoPushTokenAsync({ projectId })` uses `extra.eas.projectId`, which the app does not have yet.
  - Taps: `getLastNotificationResponse()` (synchronous), `addNotificationResponseReceivedListener`, `clearLastNotificationResponse()`.
  - Background notifications: a `TaskManager` task defined at module scope and `registerTaskAsync`, returning `BackgroundNotificationTaskResult`. It needs `enableBackgroundRemoteNotifications: true` in the plugin.
  - The payload's data may arrive as a JSON string in `data.dataString` (UNVERIFIED on a real device).
  - Push works only in a development build.
- **expo-file-system 57.0.7.** `File.downloadFileAsync(url, destination, { headers, idempotent })`.
- **expo-video 57.0.5.** `useVideoPlayer({ uri })` and `VideoView` with `allowsPictureInPicture={false}`.
- **Photo conversion.** JPEG re-encoding goes through the existing image-manipulator call chain (`expoPosterMaker`), which comes out upright on iOS.

**Rules for every P16 task.**
- Every original request, upload and sync payload goes through `leavesDevice` on both sides (rule 8). A test shows that a `localOnly` moment is never requested, kept or uploaded.
- Push payloads carry ids and fixed words only, never a transcript, a caption, a note, a title or a person's name.
- Missing stays missing (rule 10): an original that did not arrive is left out, and a token that does not exist is not invented.
- Every Workflow step is safe to replay.
- Every user-facing word lives in `packages/story/words` and follows DESIGN §8.

**Out of scope.**
- The poster and the Premiere transition (a polish task after P16).
- Edits and re-renders (P17), sharing and crew (P18–P19).
- A retry for a failed week.
- Push receipts beyond clearing dead tokens.
- An admin view of the metrics.
- Changing the episode day or hour in Settings (the stored values are used).
- Stripping location metadata from video originals (a device-check item).

## T-017a · The week and the schedule · needs T-016a

**Goal.** Pure functions say which week an episode covers, when its run starts, renders and delivers in any time zone, and how this week stands for the phone.

**Do.**

1. `packages/story/src/schedule.ts`:
   - `episodeWeek(deliveryDate)` → `{ weekStart: deliveryDate − 7, weekEnd: deliveryDate − 1 }`.
   - `episodeTimes(documentary, deliveryDate)` → `{ startAt, renderAt, deliverAt }` as UTC instants, by the "When it runs" line.
     - Time-zone maths uses `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, as `localDay` in `shareable.ts` does.
     - A local time that does not exist (spring forward) moves to the first valid minute after it. A repeated one (fall back) uses the first occurrence.
   - `dueWeeks(documentaries, now)` → `{ documentaryId, deliveryDate, weekStart, weekEnd, startAt, renderAt, deliverAt }[]`.
     - For each documentary, the candidate delivery dates are the documentary's local yesterday, today and tomorrow that fall on its `episodeDay`.
     - A candidate D is due when `startAt(D)` ≤ `now` < `deliverAt(D)` + 3 h.
   - `weekStatus(documentary, now, summary?)` → `'none' | 'making' | 'ready' | 'late' | 'failed' | 'quiet'` for the most recent delivery date whose `startAt` has passed, by the "Watching" line. It is `none` before any week has started.
   - Only `@life/contracts` and the existing date helpers are used.
2. `episodeWords` gains:
   - `ready(n)` → "Episode n is ready";
   - `making(hour)` → "This week's episode is being made. It'll be here by 6pm." (with `hourLabel(hour)`: "6pm", "noon", "midnight");
   - `late` → "This week's episode is running late.";
   - `failed` → "This week's episode couldn't be made.";
   - `quiet` → "No episode this week.";
   - `offline` → "Plays offline";
   - the short row labels `makingLabel` "Being made", `lateLabel` "Running late", `failedLabel` "Couldn't be made";
   - `channel` "Episodes".

**Tests.**
- **`episodeWeek`:** a Sunday gives the previous Sunday to Saturday.
- **`episodeTimes`:**
  - Europe/Berlin on a normal Sunday, and on both clock-change Sundays;
  - hour 2 on Berlin's spring-forward Sunday delivers at 03:00 CEST;
  - Asia/Kathmandu (+5:45), America/St_Johns (−3:30) and Pacific/Kiritimati (+14);
  - hour 5 gives `startAt` at 04:00 local, and hour 0 gives `startAt` = `renderAt` = `deliverAt` = 00:00.
- **`dueWeeks`:**
  - due at `startAt`, and not one minute before;
  - still due 2 h 59 min after `deliverAt`, and not at 3 h;
  - hour 23 is due on the next local day until 02:00 but not after;
  - hour 0 is due from 00:00;
  - not due on another weekday;
  - two documentaries in different zones at the same instant.
- **`weekStatus`:** covers each of its states.
- **Words:** the words test covers the new lines.

**Acceptance.** Story typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and the words test pass. `packages/story` imports only `@life/contracts` and `zod`.

## T-017b · Push · needs T-016d

**Goal.** The server can send a visible or a silent push to a person's phones through a port. The phone registers its push token and opens the right screen from a tap.

**Do.**

1. **Contract and storage.**
   - `RegisterDevice` gains `pushToken?`, a string of 1..200 characters matching `^Expo(nent)?PushToken\[.+\]$`.
   - The next D1 migration adds a nullable `push_token` to devices, with its migration test.
   - `devicesRepo.upsert` stores a token and clears the same token from every other device row. An absent token keeps the stored one.
   - `tokensFor(userId)` lists a user's distinct tokens.
   - The account deletion request route clears the user's tokens.
2. **The port.** `Pusher` in `ports.ts`: `send(messages: { to, title?, data: Record<string, string>, silent: boolean }[]) → { to, outcome: 'sent' | 'deviceNotRegistered' | 'failed' }[]`.
3. **The adapter.** `providers/expoPush/pusher.ts` calls the Expo endpoint with plain `fetch`, in batches of 100.
   - A visible message has `sound: 'default'`, `priority: 'high'` and `channelId: 'episodes'`. A silent one has `contentAvailable: true` and `priority: 'normal'`, with no title.
   - It sends `Authorization: Bearer` when `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` is set.
   - Tickets map to outcomes, read leniently. A non-2xx answer marks the batch `failed`, and the error message never holds a token.
4. **The fixture.** `providers/fixture/pusher.ts` records messages and answers what a test sets. `pipelineProviders` returns it with `PROVIDERS: 'fixture'`; otherwise it returns the Expo adapter.
5. **Sending.** `pipeline/deliver/push.ts` has `pushTo(ctx, userId, message)`:
   - it sends to each of the user's tokens and clears those that came back `deviceNotRegistered`;
   - it sends nothing when the user has an open deletion request.
6. **The phone: the token.**
   - `services/notifications/pushToken.ts` implements a `PushTokens` port, `current(): Promise<string | null>`. It returns `null` unless notifications are allowed (P9 asks for that permission) and `Constants.expoConfig.extra.eas.projectId` exists.
   - `application/account.ts` registers the device with the token after sign-in and on a foreground at most once a day. The day counts only after a registration that carried a token succeeded.
   - `services/notifications/channel.ts` creates the Android channel `episodes`, named `episodeWords.channel`.
7. **The phone: taps.**
   - `services/notifications/taps.ts` has `useEpisodeTaps(open)`, mounted inside `CaptureProvider`'s children once the store is open, on native only.
   - For a cold start it reads `getLastNotificationResponse()`; while running it uses the listener.
   - It navigates only once the root navigation is ready (`useRootNavigationState()?.key`), and calls `clearLastNotificationResponse()` afterwards.
   - A `data.episodeId` that is a UUID opens `/episode/{id}`. Anything else is ignored.

**Tests.**
- **API:**
  - The migration keeps earlier rows.
  - Registering the same token on a new device id removes it from the old row.
  - The adapter, against a stubbed `fetch`:
    - 150 messages make two calls;
    - the visible and the silent bodies have the fields above;
    - the bearer header is sent only with the secret;
    - a `DeviceNotRegistered` ticket maps to its outcome;
    - a 500 marks the batch `failed`, with no token in the error.
  - `pushTo` clears a dead token and sends nothing after a deletion request.
  - The account deletion request clears the tokens.
- **Mobile:**
  - No token without a project id or without permission.
  - The day counts only after a successful registration with a token.
  - `useEpisodeTaps`, with `renderRouter` and a mock `episode/[id]` route, opens it for a good id and ignores a bad one.

**Acceptance.** API and mobile typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and `deploy:dry` pass. UNVERIFIED until the owner's Expo project and a real device: a real token, a delivered push, and a tap from a killed app.

## T-017c · Asking for originals, on the server · needs T-014c, T-016d, T-017b

**Goal.** After planning, the server keeps the chosen answers, records which photos and clips it needs at full quality, lets those uploads in, and tells the run when all have arrived.

**Do.**

1. **The request contract and `leavesDevice`.**
   - `OriginalRequest { id, episodeId, documentaryId, momentId, assetId, state: 'open' | 'met' | 'closed', createdAt, updatedAt }` joins the pulled sync entities (pull only, like `derived`).
   - `leavesDevice(moment, { cloudBackup, requested })`: an asset with an open request may also upload `original`. Every existing call passes `requested`, false where it does not apply.
2. **Storage.**
   - The next D1 migration adds `original_requests`, unique on `(episode_id, asset_id)` and cascading with the episode, with its migration test.
   - `data/repositories/originalRequests.ts` has:
     - `open(episodeId, rows)`, an upsert that keeps a met row met;
     - `forEpisode`;
     - `openForAsset(assetId)`;
     - `meet(assetId)`, which returns the episodes whose last open request it met;
     - `closeAll(episodeId)`.
   - Each write adds a change-log row.
3. **`keepAnswers(episode)`.** For every answer moment the current plan uses (cold open, shots, closing) that the rebuilt brief still has, it copies `tmp/…/{asset}/answer` to `u/…/{asset}/original` in R2, streaming, with the same content type.
   - It does nothing when the original already exists or the working copy is missing.
   - It returns the ids it kept.
4. **`requestOriginals(episode)`.** For each photo or clip moment the plan uses that the rebuilt brief still has, and whose original is not in R2, it opens a request and returns the count. When it opened any, it sends one silent push `{ type: 'originals' }` through `pushTo`.
5. **The upload route.**
   - `original` is accepted for an asset with an open request, or when Cloud backup allows it. `uploadsAllowed` passes `requested` through `leavesDevice`.
   - Completing an `original` upload calls `meet(assetId)`. For each episode whose requests are now all met, it sends `originals-ready` to `ep-{documentaryId}-{weekStart}` through `env.EPISODE_PIPELINE.get(id).sendEvent`, ignoring an instance that no longer exists.
6. **Closing.** `closeAll(episodeId)` also aborts any open multipart `original` upload for those assets and removes its upload row.
7. **Moment deletion.** When `/sync` accepts a moment tombstone, it deletes the moment's `u/…/{asset}/original` from R2 and closes the asset's open requests in the same flow. The derived rows are already deleted there.

**Tests** (Workers pool, D1 and R2 in Miniflare, the fixture pusher):
- **Keeping answers:**
  - Two chosen answers with working copies get originals with the same bytes and content type.
  - An unchosen answer does not.
  - An answer made `localOnly` or deleted after planning does not.
  - A second run changes nothing.
- **Requests:**
  - Rows open for the plan's photos and clips that have no original. None open for answers, or for a clip whose original exists.
  - One silent push goes out, with no data but `type: 'originals'`.
  - A moment made `localOnly` after planning gets no request.
- **Uploads:**
  - An original for a requested asset is accepted, and one for an unrequested asset without Cloud backup is refused.
  - Completing the last one sends `originals-ready`, checked through a stub binding.
- **Sync and closing:**
  - A pull returns the requests.
  - `closeAll` sends them closed and aborts an open upload.
- **Deletion:** deleting a moment whose answer was kept removes its original.
- **`leavesDevice`:** it allows `original` for a requested photo and never for a `localOnly` one, in the story function and in the policy.

**Allowed test changes.** The existing `leavesDevice` and `uploadsAllowed` tests pass `requested: false` where they call the new signature.

**Acceptance.** Contracts, story and API typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and `deploy:dry` pass.

## T-017d · Asking for originals, on the phone · needs T-017b, T-017c

**Goal.** The phone sends what the episode asked for as soon as it can: when it is opened, from a silent push, or in the background task.

**Do.**

1. **Storing requests.**
   - The next device migration adds `original_requests` with the contract's fields. It has no foreign key to `episodes`.
   - The same migration clears `sync.cursor`, so rows pulled before this version are pulled again. It has a migration test.
   - `syncNow` lands pulled requests only for moments on this phone. A closed request is stored as closed.
2. **Queueing originals.** `application/originals.ts` has `queueRequestedOriginals()`. For each open request whose asset is on this phone and whose moment `leavesDevice` allows with `requested: true`:
   - a photo is re-encoded to a JPEG copy, in the foreground only; in the background the job waits;
   - it enqueues an `original` upload in the P6 queue, where video keeps its Wi-Fi rule;
   - an asset already queued or uploaded is skipped.

   A closed request:
   - removes its `original` job whatever its state, together with the JPEG copy, unless Cloud backup allows the original;
   - calls `DELETE /uploads/:assetId/original` for a started one.

   A refusal (403) of an `original` part is terminal for that job. It runs after every sync and at every run of the P6 background task.
3. **Silent pushes.**
   - `services/notifications/backgroundTask.ts` defines, at module scope, a `TaskManager` task for background notifications and calls `registerTaskAsync`. `composition/background.ts` imports it.
   - It ignores payloads with an `actionIdentifier`. It parses the data (the object, or `JSON.parse(data.dataString)`) with a small schema, and acts only on `{ type: 'originals' }`. Anything else returns `NoData`.
   - It runs through the P6 runner in `application/backgroundRun.ts`: the mounted runner when screens are up, otherwise the standalone one, with the same lock so it never overlaps the P6 task.
   - Within 25 s of the task's start, it syncs and queues requested originals. It then drains the upload queue for whatever time is left, skipping the drain when under 5 s remain. It returns `NewData`, `NoData` or `Failed`.
4. **The plugin option.** `app.json` sets `enableBackgroundRemoteNotifications: true` for `expo-notifications`.

**Tests.**
- **Migration:** it keeps earlier rows and clears the cursor.
- **Pulling requests:** one for a moment on the phone lands; one for a moment not on the phone does not.
