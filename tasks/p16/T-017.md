# T-017 · P16 Delivery

Text for T-017a–g. Published 2026-09-27. After publication this file does not change; fix lists go in `tasks/TASKS.md` under the task.

## Preamble

**Goal of P16.** Every week, on its own, each documentary gets its episode on the day and hour the person chose, and the phone tells them it is ready.

The whole weekly run:
- A 15-minute cron starts each due week.
- `EpisodePipeline` runs understand → plan → narrate. Then it keeps the chosen answers, asks the phone for the full photos and clips the plan uses, waits for them until shortly before the hour, renders (P15), and publishes.
- The phone uploads what was asked for, receives "Episode N is ready", and plays the episode on an Episode screen, also offline.
- Each run records how long every stage took, so delivery on time can be measured.

**Decided here (DECISIONS D42).**
- **Which week an episode covers.**
  - An episode delivered on date D (the documentary's `episodeDay`) covers the seven local days D−7 to D−1: `weekStart` = D−7 and `weekEnd` = D−1.
  - So for Sunday delivery the week runs Sunday to Saturday. The delivery day's own moments go to next week's episode: nothing is lost and nothing counts twice, and every day in the week is over before the run starts.
  - The week's moments are chosen by local day, as `weekBrief` already does.
- **When it runs.**
  - The run for date D starts at 06:00 local on D (`startAt`).
  - It renders at `episodeHour` − 15 minutes (`renderAt`).
  - It publishes at `episodeHour` (`deliverAt`, 18:00 by default).
  - Six o'clock gives understanding up to two hours for caption batches (D38) and leaves the phone most of the day to send what the plan asks for.
  - A cron runs every 15 minutes (`*/15 * * * *`, UTC; every real offset ends in :00, :30 or :45). It starts every week whose `startAt` has passed while `deliverAt` + 3 h has not, and whose episode does not exist yet. It uses `createBatch` on the instance id `ep-{documentaryId}-{weekStart}`, which skips instances that already exist, so a missed tick or a second tick starts nothing twice.
- **The order of the run.**
  - understand → plan → keep the chosen answers → delete the working copies → narrate → ask for originals → wait → render → publish.
  - Working copies are now deleted after planning instead of at the end of understanding. The chosen answers must be kept first, and nothing else changes for the rest (D38's lifecycle backstop still applies).
  - Narration runs before the wait because it needs no originals.
- **Answers need no second upload.**
  - An answer is uploaded as recorded (D37), so its working copy is the original.
  - For every answer the plan uses (cold open or shot), the run copies `tmp/…/{asset}/answer` to `u/…/{asset}/original`, streaming from R2 to R2. After that, all working copies are deleted as before.
  - This matches VISION §8: only the full recordings chosen for the episode stay in the cloud.
- **Photos and clips are asked for.**
  - For each photo or clip the plan uses whose original is not already in R2, the run writes an `OriginalRequest` row.
  - The phone learns about requests through `/sync` (pull only). `leavesDevice` gains `requested`, so an asked-for original may leave the phone without Cloud backup (rule 8, on both sides), and the upload route accepts `original` only for an asset with an open request, or with Cloud backup.
  - Photos go up as JPEG (the phone converts HEIC in the foreground, since a full-size decode needs hundreds of MB). Video still waits for Wi-Fi (D37).
  - When the last open request of an episode is met, the upload route sends the Workflow event `originals-ready`.
- **Reaching the phone.** Three routes, best first:
  - the app being opened (sync runs on foreground);
  - a silent push when the requests are written (best effort: iOS allows two or three an hour, gives 30 s, and drops them after a force-quit);
  - the background task from P6.

  Nothing depends on any one of them. At `renderAt` the run renders with what arrived. An original that did not arrive leaves its shot out (rule 10, T-016d), and the person's answers still carry the episode.
- **Delivery.**
  - After a successful render, the run sleeps until `deliverAt` (or not at all if that has passed), sets the episode `ready` with `deliveredAt`, and sends a visible push: "Episode 12 is ready" with the episode's title, opening the episode.
  - If the model plan's render fails, the run stores the recap plan as the next plan version and renders that (D20).
  - If the recap render fails too, the episode is `failed`, an error is logged, and the app shows it as running late.
  - An empty week ends quietly (D2).
- **Push.**
  - Expo's push service is called with plain `fetch` from a provider, with no SDK.
  - Tokens come from `getExpoPushTokenAsync({ projectId })` and are registered with the device (`RegisterDevice.pushToken?`).
  - A `DeviceNotRegistered` result clears that token. The optional `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` is a Worker secret.
  - With no project id (before the owner runs `eas init`) or no permission, no token is sent, and nothing else changes.
- **Watching.**
  - Episodes sync down to the phone (pull only), with no storage keys.
  - The video comes from `GET /episodes/:id/video`: session auth, the owner's documentary only, byte ranges (iOS needs them), and `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. No R2 credentials are needed on this path.
  - For offline viewing, the phone downloads each ready episode on Wi-Fi into its own files and plays the local copy. It keeps the four newest, and deletes older ones and replaced render versions.
  - `expo-video`'s own cache stores only what was played and is keyed by URL, so it is not used.
- **Measuring.** One `episode_runs` row per episode records `startedAt`, the time each stage ended, `dueAt` (= `deliverAt`), `deliveredAt`, the outcome and the counts of originals asked for and received. D1 rows can be tested and queried; Analytics Engine writes nothing locally. The targets are VISION §9's: 95% by the hour, 99% within three hours.

**Stack (checked 2026-09-27).**

- **expo-video 57.0.5:** `useVideoPlayer({ uri, headers })`, `VideoView` with `allowsPictureInPicture={false}`.

## T-017a · The week and the schedule · needs nothing

**Goal.** Pure functions say which week an episode covers and when its run starts, renders and delivers, in any time zone.

**Do.**
1. `packages/story/src/schedule.ts`:
   - `episodeWeek(deliveryDate)` → `{ weekStart: deliveryDate − 7, weekEnd: deliveryDate − 1 }`.
   - `episodeTimes(documentary, deliveryDate)` → `{ startAt, renderAt, deliverAt }` as UTC instants. They are 06:00, `episodeHour`:00 − 15 minutes and `episodeHour`:00, local in the documentary's time zone on `deliveryDate`.
     - A local time that does not exist (a spring-forward gap) moves to the first valid minute after it. A repeated one (fall back) uses the first occurrence.
     - When `episodeHour` is before 07:00, `startAt` is `deliverAt` − 60 minutes instead.
   - `dueWeeks(documentaries, now)` → one `{ documentaryId, deliveryDate, weekStart, weekEnd, startAt, renderAt, deliverAt }` for each documentary whose local date at `now` is its `episodeDay` and where `startAt` ≤ `now` < `deliverAt` + 3 h.
   - Only `@life/contracts` and the existing date helpers are used; time-zone maths uses `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, as `dates.ts` does.
2. `episodeWords` gains:
   - `ready(n)` → "Episode n is ready";
   - `making` → "This week's episode is being made. It'll be here this evening.";
   - `late` → "This week's episode is running late.";
   - `quiet` → "No episode this week. A quiet week is fine.";
   - `offline` → "Saved on this phone".

**Tests.**
- **`episodeWeek`:** a Sunday gives the previous Sunday to Saturday.
- **`episodeTimes`:**
  - Europe/Berlin on a normal Sunday, and on the Sundays of both clock changes (the 06:00 start and the 17:45 render land correctly);
  - Asia/Kathmandu (+5:45);
  - America/St_Johns (−3:30);
  - Pacific/Kiritimati (+14);
  - an hour of 5 gives `startAt` at 04:00 local.
- **`dueWeeks`:**
  - due at `startAt`, and not one minute before;
  - still due 2 h 59 min after `deliverAt`, and not at 3 h;
  - not due on another weekday;
  - two documentaries in different zones at the same instant.
- The words test covers the new lines.

**Acceptance.** Story typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and the words test pass. `packages/story` imports only `@life/contracts` and `zod`.

## T-017b · Push · needs nothing

**Goal.** The server can send a visible or a silent push to a person's phones through a port, and the phone registers its push token and opens the right screen from a tap.

**Do.**
1. **Contract and storage.**
   - `RegisterDevice` gains `pushToken?` (a string 1..200 matching `^ExponentPushToken\[.+\]$`).
   - The next D1 migration adds a nullable `push_token` to devices, with its migration test.
   - `devicesRepo.upsert` stores it (an absent token keeps the stored one), and `tokensFor(userId)` lists a user's tokens.
2. **The port.** `Pusher` in `ports.ts`: `send(messages: { to, title?, body?, data: Record<string, string>, silent: boolean }[]) → { to, outcome: 'sent' | 'deviceNotRegistered' | 'failed' }[]`.
3. **The Expo adapter.** `providers/expoPush/pusher.ts`:
   - It is plain `fetch` to the Expo endpoint, in batches of 100.
   - A visible message sends `sound: 'default'` and `priority: 'high'`. A silent one sends `_contentAvailable: true` and `priority: 'normal'`, with no title or body.
   - It adds `Authorization: Bearer` when `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` is set.
   - It maps each ticket to an outcome, and parses leniently.
   - A non-2xx answer marks the batch `failed`, and its message never holds a token.
4. **The fixture.** `providers/fixture/pusher.ts` records the messages and answers what a test sets. `pipelineProviders` returns the pusher: the fixture with `PROVIDERS: 'fixture'`, otherwise the Expo adapter, which needs no key.
5. **Sending from the pipeline.** `pipeline/deliver/push.ts` has `pushTo(ctx, userId, message)`, which sends to every token of the user and clears the tokens that came back `deviceNotRegistered`.
6. **The phone: the token.** `apps/mobile/src/services/notifications/pushToken.ts`, called after sign-in and on each foreground, at most once a day:
   - It asks for the token only when notifications are already allowed (P9 asks for that permission) and `Constants.expoConfig.extra.eas.projectId` exists.
   - It registers the device with the token.
   - Any failure leaves the device registered without a token.
7. **The phone: taps.** In `_layout.tsx`, a notification tap (cold start through `getLastNotificationResponse()`, warm through the listener) whose `data.episodeId` is a UUID opens `/episode/{id}`. Any other data is ignored.

**Tests.**
- **API:**
  - The migration keeps earlier rows, and a device registered twice keeps one row whose token updates.
  - The adapter against a stubbed `fetch`:
    - a batch of 150 messages makes two calls;
    - a visible and a silent body have the fields above;
    - the bearer header appears only with the secret;
    - a `DeviceNotRegistered` ticket maps to its outcome;
    - a 500 answer marks the batch `failed`, with no token in the error.
  - `pushTo` clears a dead token.
  - No message in any test carries text other than our words and ids.
- **Mobile:**
  - No token is asked for without a project id or without permission.
  - A tap with `{ episodeId }` opens the episode route (rendered router test).
  - A tap with a bad id does nothing.

**Acceptance.** API and mobile typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and `deploy:dry` pass. UNVERIFIED until the owner's Expo project and a real device: a real token, a delivered push, and a tap from a killed app.

## T-017c · Asking for originals, on the server · needs T-014c, T-016d, T-017b

**Goal.** After planning, the server keeps the chosen answers, records which photos and clips it needs at full quality, lets those uploads in, and tells the run when they have all arrived.

**Do.**
1. **Contracts and the rule.**
   - `OriginalRequest { id, episodeId, documentaryId, momentId, assetId, state: 'open' | 'met' | 'closed', createdAt, updatedAt }` joins the pulled sync entities (pull only, like `derived`).
   - `leavesDevice(moment, { cloudBackup, requested })`: an asset with an open request may also upload `original`. `requested` defaults to false, and every existing call passes it.
   - The next D1 migration adds `original_requests` (unique `(episode_id, asset_id)`, cascade with the episode), with its migration test.
   - `data/repositories/originalRequests.ts` has `open(episodeId, rows)` (an upsert that keeps a met row met), `forEpisode`, `openForAsset(assetId)`, `meet(assetId)` (returns the episodes whose last open request it met) and `closeAll(episodeId)`. Each write adds a change-log row, so the phone pulls it.
2. **`keepAnswers(episode)`.** For every answer moment the current plan uses (cold open and shots), it copies `tmp/…/{asset}/answer` to `u/…/{asset}/original` in R2, streaming the body with the same content type. It does nothing when the original already exists or the working copy is missing, and returns the ids it kept.
3. **`requestOriginals(episode)`.**
   - For each photo or clip moment the plan uses, that the rebuilt brief still has, and whose original is not in R2, it opens a request and returns their count.
   - When it opened any, it sends one silent push `{ type: 'originals' }` through `pushTo`.
4. **Uploads.**
   - The upload route accepts `original` for an asset that has an open request, or when Cloud backup allows it. `uploadsAllowed` passes `requested` through `leavesDevice`.
   - Completing an `original` upload calls `meet(assetId)`. For each episode whose requests are now all met, it sends `originals-ready` to the instance `ep-{documentaryId}-{weekStart}` with `env.EPISODE_PIPELINE.get(id).sendEvent`. It ignores an instance that no longer exists.
5. **Closing.** `closeAll(episodeId)` runs at render time, so the phone stops trying.

**Tests** (Workers pool, D1 and R2 in Miniflare, the fixture pusher):
- **`keepAnswers`:** two chosen answers with working copies get originals with the same bytes and content type; an unchosen answer does not; a second run changes nothing.
- **`requestOriginals`:**
  - opens rows for the plan's photos and clips that have no original, and none for answers or for a clip whose original exists;
  - sends one silent push whose data holds no text but `originals`;
  - a moment made `localOnly` after planning gets no request.
- **Upload and pull:**
  - uploading an original for a requested asset is accepted, and for an unrequested one without Cloud backup it is refused;
  - completing the last one sends `originals-ready`, checked through the Workflow introspector or a stub binding;
  - a pull returns the requests, and `closeAll` sends them closed.
- **`leavesDevice`:** it allows `original` for a requested photo and never for a `localOnly` one, on both the story and the policy side.

**Acceptance.** Contracts, story and API typecheck, tests, lint, format, boundaries and `deploy:dry` pass.

## T-017d · Asking for originals, on the phone · needs T-017b, T-017c

**Goal.** The phone sends what the episode asked for as soon as it can: on opening, from a silent push, or in the background task.

**Do.**
1. **Storing requests.**
   - The next device migration adds `original_requests` (the contract's fields), with its migration test.
   - `syncNow` lands pulled requests only for moments on this phone. A closed one is stored as closed.
2. **`apps/mobile/src/application/originals.ts`:** `queueRequestedOriginals()`. For each open request whose asset is on this phone and whose moment `leavesDevice` allows with `requested: true`:
   - a photo that is not JPEG is converted to a JPEG copy first, and only in the foreground (the job waits otherwise);
   - it enqueues an `original` upload in the P6 queue (video keeps its Wi-Fi rule);
   - an asset already queued or uploaded is skipped.
   - A closed request removes its queued job if it has not started.
3. **When it runs.** It runs after every sync and at each run of the P6 background task.
4. **Silent pushes.**
   - `services/notifications/backgroundTask.ts` defines a `TaskManager` task for background notifications, registered with `registerTaskAsync`.
   - On `{ type: 'originals' }` it syncs, queues requested originals, and drains the upload queue for at most 25 s. It returns `NewData`, `NoData` or `Failed`.
   - It opens the store the way the P6 background task does, and never at the same time as that task (one shared lock).
5. **Config.** `app.json` sets `enableBackgroundRemoteNotifications: true` for `expo-notifications`.

**Tests.**
- The migration keeps earlier rows.
- A pulled request for a moment on the phone lands; one for a moment not on the phone does not.
- **`queueRequestedOriginals`:**
  - it enqueues a JPEG photo and a clip;
  - it converts a HEIC photo in the foreground, and leaves it waiting in the background;
  - it skips a `localOnly` moment and an already-queued asset;
  - a closed request removes its unstarted job.
- The silent-push task, with fakes, syncs, queues and drains within its budget, and never overlaps the background task (the lock test).
- No upload body ever carries a `localOnly` moment's file (checked on the fake).

**Acceptance.** Mobile typecheck, tests, lint, format and boundaries pass. UNVERIFIED until a real device: a silent push waking the app, uploads from the background task, and HEIC conversion memory on a 48 MP photo.

## T-017e · The weekly run · needs T-015c, T-016d, T-017a, T-017c

**Do.**
1. **The cron.**

   - `pipeline/schedule.ts` has `startDueWeeks(env, now)`. It reads the documentaries, applies `dueWeeks`, drops weeks whose episode row already exists, and calls `createBatch` in groups of 100 with the instance id and the week's times as params.
2. **The reordered `EpisodePipeline`:**
   - the understand steps, without deletion;
   - `plan` and `plan-recap` as today, with the empty week ending the run;

   - the narrate steps (P14);
   - `request-originals`;

   - on failure: `recap-store` (`recapPlan` as the next plan version, createdBy `recap`, state `rendering`) and a second render the same way. If that fails too: `failed`, with an error logged;
   - `deliver-wait` (`sleepUntil(deliverAt)` only when it is in the future);

   - `run-record`.
3. **Measuring.**
   - The next D1 migration adds `episode_runs`: `episode_id`, `started_at`, `understood_at`, `planned_at`, `narrated_at`, `render_started_at`, `delivered_at`, `due_at`, `outcome` (`model` / `recap` / `empty` / `failed`), `originals_asked` and `originals_received`. It has its migration test.
   - Each stage writes its time as it ends. `episodes.ts` gains `setDelivered`.
4. **Architecture.** In ARCHITECTURE §5 "Episode", update the flow line to this order, the times and D42.

**Tests** (Workers pool; fixture transcriber, captioner, planner, narrator, renderer and pusher; sleeps disabled):

- **The Workflow:**
  - a model week runs to `ready` with one visible push whose data has only the episode id, the words "Episode n is ready" and the plan's title, and an `episode_runs` row with every time and the outcome `model`;
  - the chosen answers' originals exist and every working copy is gone;
  - with `mockEvent('originals-ready')` the render starts without waiting; with `forceEventTimeout` it renders with what arrived, and a clip whose original never came is left out of the manifest;
  - a renderer failing the model render renders the recap, stored as plan version 2, and ends `ready` with outcome `recap`;
  - failing both ends `failed` with no push;
  - an empty week ends with no render and no push;
  - a replayed `publish` step sends no second push.

## T-017f · Episodes to the phone · needs T-016d, T-017e

**Goal.** The phone knows its episodes and can stream or download the video privately.

**Do.**
1. **The pulled row.** `EpisodeSummary { id, documentaryId, number, weekStart, weekEnd, state, title?, durationMs?, renderVersion, dueAt?, deliveredAt?, updatedAt }` joins the pulled sync entities (pull only). It has no storage keys.
   - `title` comes from the current plan.
   - `dueAt` comes from `episode_runs`.
   - A change log row is written whenever the episode's state, plan or render changes.
2. **The video route.** `GET /episodes/:id/video` (session):
